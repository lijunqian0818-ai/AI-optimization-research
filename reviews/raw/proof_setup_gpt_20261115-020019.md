1. **Remark 4: the conclusion that only a never-trading policy can have a universal guarantee is too strong.**

   **Location:** `theorems.md`, Remark 4; `proofs.md` §4.

   **Why wrong:** The all-loss environment proves that positive trading probability and low risk cannot both be guaranteed **in every environment**. It does not prove that a universally safe policy must abstain in every environment. The sentence “a policy cannot know in advance which environment it is in” introduces an information restriction absent from A1–A4.

   For example, the allowed filtration may already reveal candidate labels. A policy that trades only candidates whose revealed label is zero has \(r_T^{entry}=R_T=0\) whenever it trades, in every environment. It abstains in the all-loss environment but trades in others.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** State only:
   > No policy can guarantee, in every A1–A4 environment, both positive probability of trading and positive probability of meeting the risk threshold while trading.

   Delete the global “only policy … never trades” conclusion and the unsupported information argument. A stronger impossibility result requires an explicit indistinguishability restriction.

2. **The deadline interval does not establish a permanent identification limit.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.2; `theorems.md`, Corollary 1.3; `proofs.md` §1.8.

   **Why wrong:** The assertion that the deadline-unsold component “does not shrink with more data” is not implied by the assumptions. Its fraction can converge to zero—for example, if only finitely many positions miss their deadlines. Moreover, liquidation outcomes may become observable after the deadline. A fixed-deadline procedure deliberately discards that later information.

   The underlying conditional gap is
   \[
   \frac{\sum_{t\le T}
   E[X_t^{hi}-X_t^{lo}\mid F_t]}{N_T}
   =
   \frac{\sum_{t\le T}E[U_t^D\mid F_t]}{N_T},
   \]
   where \(U_t^D\) counts entries unsold at their deadlines. The observed count \(K_T^{hi}-K_T^{lo}\) is not this conditional gap.

   **Fixable:** Yes, as an interpretation correction.

   **Suggested fix:** Describe the construction as a conservative interval based on deadline-coarsened outcomes. State that its deadline-unsold component **can persist** if a positive fraction misses the deadline. A claim of unavoidable nonidentification requires specifying the available observations and proving observational equivalence of environments with different liquidation targets.

3. **Corollary 1.3 needs synchronized revelation and explicit observable accounting.**

   **Location:** `theorems.md`, Corollary 1.3 assumptions and definition of \(P_T\); `proofs.md` §1.8.

   **Why incomplete:** The proof treats both labels as having the same revealed-entry set. Without this, \(K_T^{hi}-K_T^{lo}\) need not count deadline-unsold entries.

   For example, an eventual loss sold before the deadline has \(L^{lo}=L^{hi}=1\). Revealing only its upper label gives \(K^{hi}-K^{lo}=1\), although it was not unsold at its deadline.

   A3 also ensures measurability of aggregate counts, not the individual bookkeeping required by the stated \(K\)'s and \(P\).

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Require an A4 analogue with a common revealed set \(J_T\): both deadline labels are disclosed together for entries in \(J_T\), and
   \[
   P_T=N_T-|J_T|.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   K_T^{hi}-K_T^{lo}
   =\sum_{(t,i)\in J_T}
   1\{\text{entry unsold at its deadline}\}.
   \]
   This is a fraction of **all entries** after division by \(N_T\), not the fraction among resolved entries.

4. **Increasing the lag need not widen the computed interval.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.3, “over-stating \(d\) … only widens the interval.”

   **Why wrong:** Increasing \(d\) changes the residue-class partition. The resulting radii are not pathwise monotone in \(d\).

   For example, let the only entries through \(T=4\) occur on days 1 and 4, with one entry each. Then
   \[
   B_T(d=2)=2\sqrt{\log(4c_0/\delta)},
   \]
   whereas
   \[
   B_T(d=3)=\sqrt{2\log(24c_0/\delta)}.
   \]
   At \(\delta=0.05\), these are approximately \(4.42\) and \(3.65\), respectively: the radius decreases.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Say that a larger valid lag preserves coverage and generally worsens the worst-case rate bound. Do not claim nested intervals or pathwise monotonicity. The heuristic \(\sqrt{d'/d}\) also omits the change in logarithmic factors.

5. **The closing-auction lag convention conflicts with the adopted filtration.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §§3.1 and 3.3, third row of the lag table.

   **Why wrong:** Section 3.1 places \(F_t\) after fills and fill prices are known. The table’s third row instead uses information before the closing price is known. For a closing-auction fill at the closing price, these are different information sets.

   Under the adopted post-fill convention, the submission-time information restriction does not itself force \(d=H+1\).

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Distinguish submission information from post-fill information. If the official mark is available at the post-fill point, use \(d=H\). If it is published later, specify that timing and use a suitable larger lag. Remove the inconsistent row or label it as a different, submission-based setup.

   Also, “\(d=H+1\) is always safe” requires the explicit operational assumption that the mark and all label inputs are available by the next decision point. It does not follow merely from the label being economically determined at the close.

6. **The total-width rate is overstated without controlling pending labels.**

   **Location:** Theorem 1’s plain-language interpretation; Proposition 2’s “Scope”; `proofs.md` §2.5.

   **Why incomplete:** The displayed upper bound is
   \[
   \operatorname{width}(I_T)
   \le
   \frac{P_T}{N_T}
   +2\sqrt{\frac{dM\ell_T}{N_T}}.
   \]
   Thus the interval’s total width is not generally of order \(\sqrt{dM/N_T}\), even up to logarithms, unless the pending term is controlled.

   A3 concerns \(X_t\), the aggregate loss count. It does not explicitly require every individual label to be disclosed by \(t+d\). Consequently, the general assumptions do not imply \(P_T\le dM\).

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Either:

   - describe the rate as the **sampling-error contribution**, retaining \(P_T/N_T\); or
   - require individual-label revelation by \(t+d\), obtaining
     \[
     \operatorname{width}(I_T)
     \le \frac{dM}{N_T}
     +2\sqrt{\frac{dM\ell_T}{N_T}};
     \]
   - alternatively, build the accounting bracket using known mature aggregate counts.

   Qualify the upper/lower rate comparison by its sample-size regime and the trivial width cap of 1.

7. **The auxiliary Hoeffding lemma omits an integrability qualification.**

   **Location:** `proofs.md` §1.2; repeated claims that the cap \(M\) is unused.

   **Why incomplete:** The lemma allows an arbitrary random finite bound \(\nu\), without assuming integrability of \(X\). Although a nonnegative extended conditional expectation exists, the proof then uses the ordinary signed conditional expectation
   \[
   E[\mu-X\mid\mathcal G]=0.
   \]
   This is not automatically justified under the usual integrable-variable definition.

   The stated theorem avoids this problem because A1 supplies \(X_t\le M\). Thus the cap is used implicitly to justify the proof’s ordinary conditional-expectation manipulations, even though it does not enter the boundary formula.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Add \(E[X]<\infty\) to the auxiliary lemma. For a genuinely cap-free theorem with potentially nonintegrable counts, explicitly use localization on \(\{\nu\le r\}\) and establish the normalized conditional exponential bound there before passing to the limit.

8. **A stop-loss or take-profit does not generally reveal a fixed-horizon marked label early.**

   **Location:** `setup.md`, A4 reasonableness paragraph.

   **Why wrong:** A stop-loss or take-profit event can determine an actual-exit label, but the price at the fixed future marking horizon can subsequently reverse. Such an event does not ordinarily determine O1.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Remove this example for O1, or explicitly restrict it to another fixed label convention. Early revelation must disclose the actual final label, not a proxy based on an intermediate event.

9. **Reference trading costs are computed from the wrong holdings difference.**

   **Location:** `proofs.md` §3.2, item 2.

   **Why wrong:** The expression
   \[
   \Delta h_t^E=h_t^E-h_{t-1}^E
   \]
   is not generally the dollar trade vector when \(h_t^E\) denotes dollar holdings. Existing positions change dollar value as prices move.

   For example, an unchanged share position growing from \$100 to \$110 requires zero trading, although this difference reports \$10.

   **Fixable:** Yes; the attribution identity itself only needs an assigned scalar \(C^E\).

   **Suggested fix:** Compute trades from share changes at execution prices, or subtract pre-trade marked holdings:
   \[
   \text{dollar trades}_t
   =h_t^E-h_{t^-}^E.
   \]
   Specify price evolution, corporate actions, and execution timing in the cost model.

10. **The interpretation of \(\Pi\) as a wealth change requires additional accounting assumptions.**

    **Location:** `proofs.md` §3.2, item 7.

    **Why incomplete:** Zero cash return alone does not imply that \(\Pi^a\) equals terminal minus initial wealth. External deposits or withdrawals, omitted dividends, financing charges, or inconsistent holdings timing can invalidate that equality.

    **Fixable:** Yes.

    **Suggested fix:** Require self-financing accounting with no external flows, total returns including distributions, correctly timed holdings, and all relevant costs included. Otherwise retain \(\Pi\) solely as the defined additive performance quantity.

11. **The continuity claim for \(b^*\) needs a domain restriction.**

    **Location:** `proofs.md`, “Deviations from design notes,” item 2.

    **Why incomplete:** The stated function is defined at zero and for \(q\ge1\), but not on \((0,1)\). Extending the minimum-of-lines formula to positive \(q\) yields
    \[
    \lim_{q\downarrow0}b^*(q)
    =\tfrac12\sqrt{L_0}>0,
    \]
    whereas \(b^*(0)=0\). Thus it is not continuous at zero under that extension.

    **Fixable:** Yes.

    **Suggested fix:** Say that \(b^*\) is continuous on \([1,\infty)\) and removes the dyadic-boundary jumps there.

12. **Numerical checks are presented as stronger evidence than they provide.**

    **Location:** `proofs.md` §2.3, final parenthetical; theorem status table for Theorem 1(c).

    **Why wrong:** Checking Pinsker’s inequality on finitely many parameter values does not remove the proof’s reliance on that inequality. Similarly, empirical two-sided coverage at failure level \(\delta\) does not establish either one-sided failure bound \(\delta/2\).

    **Fixable:** Yes.

    **Suggested fix:** State that these are finite numerical sanity checks only. Attribute Proposition 2 to the analytic Pinsker bound, and Theorem 1(c) to the separate signed union bounds. Verify bibliographic references separately.
