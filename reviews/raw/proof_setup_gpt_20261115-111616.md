1. **Revelation-lag characterization is too strong.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.3, the displayed “Rule,” both “Smallest valid d” tables, and “d = H + 1 is valid if and only if OA-next holds.”

   **Why wrong:** A3 requires only \(X_t\in\mathcal F_{t+d}\). Knowing every input to every individual label is sufficient, but not necessary. The aggregate loss count may already be known without those inputs. For example, if all labels are deterministically zero, A3 holds with \(d=1\), even if mark prices arrive much later. Likewise, OA-next alone establishes an upper bound on a valid lag; it does not establish minimality.

   The displayed minimum also depends on \(t\) as written, whereas A3 requires one deterministic lag valid uniformly over all days and outcomes, up to null sets.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Replace “if and only if” with “if,” and “Smallest valid d” with “Guaranteed valid lag under the stated availability assumptions.” Define an operational lag as a known deterministic \(d\ge1\) such that, **for every \(t\)**, all required inputs are \(\mathcal F_{t+d}\)-measurable. Distinguish this sufficient operational condition from A3 itself. For the permitted \(H=0\) marked variant, retain the theorem’s restriction \(d\ge1\).

2. **Unbounded liquidation time does not imply failure of A3 for the loss label.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.2, O2: “A3 fails for O2 with any fixed d”; repeated in A3’s reasonableness discussion.

   **Why wrong:** The liquidation time and the time at which its binary loss outcome becomes measurable are different objects. An arbitrarily delayed liquidation can have a loss label already known at entry. For example, an environment with \(L^{liq}_{ti}=1\) deterministically satisfies A3 with \(d=1\), regardless of liquidation delays.

   Conversely, merely completing the label definition for never-liquidated positions does not make that label observable at a finite time.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Say: “The O2 convention does not guarantee A3 for any fixed lag. Applying Theorem 1 directly to O2 requires an additional bounded-label-revelation assumption.” The deadline construction can remain unchanged.

3. **The reference-turnover formula conflates price returns with total returns and mishandles corporate actions.**

   **Location:** `theorems.md`, Lemma 3, “How \(C^E\) is to be computed”; `proofs.md` §3.2(2).

   **Why wrong:** The proof uses
   \[
   p_t=p_{t-1}\circ(1+R_{t-1}),
   \]
   which is a price-ratio identity. It generally fails for dividend-inclusive total returns or adjusted-price returns combined with unadjusted share counts.

   For example, one share falls from \$100 to \$99 upon paying a \$1 cash dividend. Its total return is zero. With no trade, the next risky holding is \$99, but using total return in the proposed formula gives
   \[
   \operatorname{trade}_t=99-100(1+0)=-1,
   \]
   falsely recording a sale. Splits also require updating share counts before applying the share-change formula.

   **Fixable:** Yes; equation (2) itself is unaffected, but modeled costs can change materially.

   **Suggested fix:** Define \(s_{t-}\) as the actual pre-trade share vector **after corporate actions**, and use
   \[
   h_{t-}=s_{t-}\circ p_t,\qquad
   \operatorname{trade}_t=(s_t-s_{t-})\circ p_t.
   \]
   Account for cash distributions separately. Use the return-based shortcut only under an explicitly compatible price/share convention. Reserve dividend-inclusive total returns for the P&L calculation.

4. **The wealth interpretation needs an explicit prohibition on double-counting slippage.**

   **Location:** `theorems.md`, Lemma 3, W2–W3; `proofs.md` §3.2(2), §3.2(7).

   **Why wrong:** W3 requires \(C\) to include slippage, while holdings and returns may already use actual execution prices. Execution-price slippage is then already reflected in P&L.

   For example, buying at \$101 and ending at \$110 produces \$9 before explicit fees. If the return is computed from the \$101 execution price, subtracting another \$1 of slippage relative to a \$100 benchmark reports \$8.

   W1–W4 as written do not explicitly exclude this inconsistent accounting.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Specify one consistent convention:
   
   - actual execution-price accounting, with \(C\) containing only costs not already embedded in holdings and returns; or
   - benchmark-price accounting, with execution-price differences explicitly reconciled through \(C\).

   State that every economic cost is counted exactly once.

5. **Zero cash return is sufficient, not necessary, for equality with wealth change.**

   **Location:** `proofs.md` §3.2(7): “Π equals the wealth change exactly when … \(\rho_t=0\).” Also “Each of W1–W3 is necessary for the recursion.”

   **Why wrong:** The displayed end-of-period-cost recursion gives
   \[
   W_{T+1}-W_1-\Pi=\sum_t\rho_tW_t.
   \]
   Equality requires this sum to vanish, not every \(\rho_t\) to vanish. Nonzero terms can cancel. Similarly, W1–W3 are sufficient structural accounting conditions; individual paths can satisfy the same identity despite violations whose effects cancel.

   In addition, \(\sum_t\rho_tW_t\) is not literally interest earned on the cash account. Actual cash interest is \(\sum_t\rho_t(W_t-\mathbf1^\top h_t)\), under the stated timing. The total-wealth term arises because \(\Pi\) uses excess returns.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Replace “exactly when” with “in particular, when,” describe W1–W3 as sufficient accounting conditions, and call \(\sum_t\rho_tW_t\) the cash-benchmark return on total wealth.

6. **The stated threshold for an informative upper bound is only sufficient.**

   **Location:** `theorems.md`, Proposition 2, Scope qualification (i); `proofs.md` §2.5.

   **Why wrong:** The text says the upper bound is informative “only when”
   \[
   N>9dM\ell_T.
   \]
   That is the threshold for the relaxed bound \(3\sqrt{dM\ell_T/N}<1\), not for the preceding, tighter bound
   \[
   \min\{1,dM/N+2\sqrt{dM\ell_T/N}\}.
   \]
   Writing \(y=dM/N\), the tighter bound is informative whenever
   \[
   y+2\sqrt{y\ell_T}<1,
   \]
   equivalently
   \[
   y<(\sqrt{\ell_T+1}-\sqrt{\ell_T})^2.
   \]
   The computed interval can also be informative when neither coarse bound establishes it.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Say: “The simplified bound \(3\sqrt{dM\ell_T/N}\) is informative when \(N>9dM\ell_T\); this is sufficient, not necessary, for the sharper bound or computed interval to be informative.”

7. **The claimed stitching penalty omits the confidence-allocation penalty.**

   **Location:** `proofs.md` §1.5: “relative to the fixed-time Hoeffding radius … stitching costs a factor between 1 and \(\sqrt2\).” Related wording in §5(6).

   **Why wrong:** This comparison holds only with the **same logarithmic quantity** \(L=L_k\). At a fixed nominal error probability, the fixed-time logarithm does not include the allocation across lanes and epochs:
   \[
   L_k=\log(2dc_0/\delta)+2\log(k+1).
   \]
   The complete comparison therefore includes a logarithmic inflation in addition to the geometric factor. A mixture boundary does not automatically eliminate the cost of time-uniform validity.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** State: “Holding \(L_k\) fixed, the dyadic radius has a geometric inflation between 1 and \(\sqrt2\) relative to \(\sqrt{qL_k/2}\). The overall penalty at a fixed nominal confidence level also includes the lane and epoch allocations.”

8. **The marked outcome is not defined for every possible auction outcome.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.2, O1’s assertion that the mark is defined for every entry.

   **Why wrong:** The listed cases cover a stock that trades at the mark and a stock that is suspended. They omit a nonsuspended stock with no transaction at the specified auction. Delisted securities and some corporate actions also need explicit valuation treatment. OA-open partially mentions “did not trade,” but the label definition itself does not consistently specify that case.

   An undefined mark leaves A2 unestablished; late discretionary valuation decisions can also invalidate the claimed lag.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Define an exhaustive, pre-specified valuation hierarchy covering no auction print, suspension, delisting, distributions, and corporate actions. Require its inputs to satisfy the selected availability assumption.

9. **The executable-exit interpretation uses an unstated execution model.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §§3.1–3.2: limit-up/down descriptions, O2’s “first opportunity,” and the assertion that O1 equals O2 for every non-blocked scheduled exit.

   **Why wrong:** “Not suspended, not locked at limit-down” does not, by itself, establish that the entire position executes at the auction price. Queue priority, available volume, order characteristics, partial fills, and position size matter. Conversely, a limit price alone does not universally determine whether a particular order can fill.

   The single binary entry indicator and single liquidation time also leave partial liquidation unspecified. These are application assumptions absent from A1–A5. They are necessary for the asserted economic interpretation, although not for concentration of an already well-defined label.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Specify a full-position execution model, or define liquidation through an observable order-and-fill policy with explicit partial-fill handling. State O1 = O2 only when the scheduled liquidation executes completely at the valuation price under identical cost accounting. Ensure the deadline labels use precisely that same liquidation policy, as required in Corollary 1.3’s proof, Step 0.

10. **The lane-filtration explanation excludes information that the assumptions permit.**

    **Location:** `proofs.md` §1.1: intervening information is contained in \(\mathcal H_m^j\) “and in no earlier σ-field of the lane filtration.”

    **Why wrong:** Some intervening decisions or outcomes may already be predictable or known in an earlier σ-field. A1–A3 impose no prohibition on this. Containment in the later σ-field does not imply exclusion from earlier ones.

    **Fixable:** Yes.

    **Suggested fix:** Delete “and in no earlier σ-field.” The argument requires only that the lane filtration has no sampled intermediate index and that centering occurs conditional on \(\mathcal F_{t_{j,m}}\).

11. **The safe-policy example can violate the daily entry cap.**

    **Location:** `theorems.md`, Remark 4, “trade exactly the candidates whose revealed label is 0”; repeated in `proofs.md` §4.

    **Why wrong:** A finite candidate set can contain more than \(M\) zero-label candidates. Trading all of them violates A1.

    **Fixable:** Yes.

    **Suggested fix:** Select at most \(M\) revealed-zero candidates using a fixed measurable ordering.

The substantive assumption concern is **A3**, a deterministic bounded-information-delay condition: it is conventional for bounded-delay models but requires independent operational justification here. Neither a fixed economic horizon nor eventual liquidation supplies it automatically. A5 adds a separate disclosure requirement. The accounting interpretation additionally requires the execution, corporate-action, and cost conventions identified above; these cannot be supplied by the probabilistic assumptions.

**Verdict: FIXABLE ERRORS**
