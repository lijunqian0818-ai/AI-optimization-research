Several points materially affect the novelty assessment:

- **Random denominators do not automatically create new theory.** For \(\theta=E[A]/E[N]\), with \(E[N]>0\) and suitable moment and dependence conditions, the influence function is \((A-\theta N)/E[N]\). A vector of such contrasts often reduces to existing multivariate mean inference. New work could arise from weak denominators, nonsmooth learning, or failure of a uniform linear approximation—but those problems must actually be addressed.
- **Fixed-b is an asymptotic approximation, not an exact finite-sample coverage guarantee.** “Finite effective block count” needs a precise asymptotic regime and assumptions. Neither changing critical values nor passing selected simulations establishes uniform validity over unknown data-generating processes.
- **The reported 9.7% noncoverage under iid sampling needs investigation first.** It does not identify temporal dependence as the cause. It could reflect calibration, studentization, ratio behavior, implementation, or the simulation design. A literature-based explanation cannot substitute for isolating that failure.
- **The simulation gate’s direction should be corrected.** The intended target is presumably *noncoverage* at most \(0.05+\delta\), not “coverage at most \(0.05+\delta\).” For independent simulation replications and known truth, certification is a binomial inference problem. Uncertain truth or adaptive reuse of simulations requires additional treatment.

A precise new coverage-error theorem for the proposed statistic could remain novel, but the draft does not yet supply it. The integrated ML stock-selection benchmark is the strongest plausible remaining contribution; I have not identified a paper duplicating that entire benchmark, but its novelty would primarily be empirical and procedural.
