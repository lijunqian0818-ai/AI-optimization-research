1. **Daily-bar execution can violate the stated information assumption.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.1 EM, §3.2 valuation rule (4), §6 items 1–3; A1.

   **Why it is wrong:** EM requires an opening-auction print. The proposed daily-bar proxy instead uses the official open when the stock traded sometime that day. If the opening auction produces no trade and the first trade occurs later, both that price and the fact that trading eventually occurred are unavailable at the opening-auction decision point. An entry indicator constructed from these inputs need not be \(F_t\)-measurable.

   Increasing the **label** lag to \(H+1\) does not repair look-ahead in the **entry** indicator. Admitting the eventual daily “open” into \(F_t\) would also contradict the stated interpretation of \(F_t\) as actual information at the auction match.

   **Fixable:** Yes, but the daily-bar implementation needs to change.

   **Suggested fix:** Use timestamped auction data for opening-auction fills, or move the entry information time to when the inputs are genuinely available and redefine the target and lag accordingly. If a daily-bar simulation is retained, explicitly distinguish its retrospective synthetic fills from executable opening-auction entries.

2. **Post-fill measurability does not establish that the trading policy is implementable.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §§1, 3.1, 6 item 1.

   **Why it is wrong:** A1 permits selections to depend on all information at the auction match. An order participating in that auction generally must have been submitted before its outcome was known. Thus A1 alone permits an algorithm to choose which orders supposedly participated after seeing their clearing prices.

   This does not invalidate the abstract concentration result, but it leaves an unstated assumption behind claims about an executable strategy.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Require submitted names, order quantities, order types and limit prices to be measurable with respect to the pre-submission information \(G_t\). Define executed entries as the resulting post-fill outcomes, measurable with respect to \(F_t\). Keep the conditional target based on \(F_t\).

3. **The valuation hierarchy is not a well-defined “first applicable level” algorithm.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.2, V1–V6 and V-CA; A2 reasonableness.

   **Why it is wrong:**

   - V1 can apply because an auction price exists while corporate-action or cost inputs are missing. Under the literal first-applicable rule, V1 wins and the final V6 fallback is never reached.
   - V6 also covers unsupported corporate actions, which can coexist with V1–V5.
   - A delisted security can satisfy an earlier “no trade” or suspension condition, preventing V5 from being reached.
   - V5 explicitly handles merger or exchange consideration, while V-CA sends conversions and other unlisted actions to V6. Their precedence is unspecified.
   - V5(a) values successor securities using only V1–V4; a successor that itself needs another delisting or unsupported-action rule has no explicit resolution.

   Consequently, the assertions that the hierarchy is exhaustive and makes A2–A3 hold “by construction” do not follow from the algorithm as written.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Specify an actual decision tree: first resolve supported security transformations and listing status; then choose the applicable price rule; finally validate all required inputs at the frozen data deadline. Missing inputs or unsupported cases must override the tentative valuation. Define how successor securities are handled, including a terminating fallback.

4. **Several unconditional lag-\(H\) claims survive despite the V2/V3 qualification.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §1 timeline; §3.2 “Why the exit-time mark,” item 3; §3.3 examples and concluding discussion; V2.

   **Why it is wrong:** OA-open supplies auction-time information. It does not reveal whether a stock with no auction print will trade later that day. The adopted hierarchy therefore can require information strictly after \(\tau_{t+H}\), even under OA-open.

   In particular, V2 says that unavailable V2 cases “fall to V3” under lag \(H\). But V3 requires **no trade all day**. An entry whose stock trades later does not satisfy V3, and the V3 predicate is itself unavailable at the opening auction.

   The qualified table row does not repair contradictory unconditional statements elsewhere.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** State that the adopted hierarchy has guaranteed lag \(H+1\), subject to the specified availability or fallback rule. A lag-\(H\) version must be a separately defined label using only auction-time information—for example, a reference-price fallback immediately whenever there is no auction print.

5. **Changing \(d\) can change the label, not merely the confidence radius.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` V6, §3.3 “A larger valid lag”; `theorems.md` Theorem 1 discussion of alternative lags.

   **Why it is wrong:** V6 defines the label using missingness at \(\tau_{t+d}\). An input missing at \(t+d\) but present at \(t+d'\) can produce a forced loss with the first deadline and a non-loss with the second. Thus recomputing the convention at a larger \(d'\) changes \(X_t\), \(\mu_t\), and the estimand.

   The implication
   \[
   X_t\in F_{t+d}\implies X_t\in F_{t+d'}
   \]
   concerns the **same frozen random variable**. It does not justify treating labels recomputed with a later fallback deadline as the same analysis.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Introduce a separate fixed label-finalization deadline, say \(d_{\mathrm{label}}\). Freeze labels there. Any later monitoring lag can then be varied while retaining the same labels and target.

6. **Dividend accounting is inconsistent between O1 and O2.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.2, O1 position value, O2 label, and “When O1 = O2”; A4-D reasonableness.

   **Why it is wrong:** O1 includes distributions according to their **ex-date**, whereas O2 includes cash distributions “received while it was held.” Payment can occur after the position has been sold.

   For example, a position bought for 100 becomes entitled to a dividend of 10, sells for 95, and receives the dividend after sale. O1 counts proceeds of 105. The literal O2 receipt-while-held rule counts only 95. Full execution at the mark does not eliminate this difference.

   The equality statement conditions on identical corporate-action treatment, but the supplied definitions do not actually impose identical treatment. Consequently, the assertion that the EM sell-fill event is the equality event is too strong.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Use a common entitlement-based convention: include all distributions attributable to the position while held, regardless of payment date. Specify taxes and receivables consistently. If their final amounts are not known at sale, do not claim that liquidation automatically reveals the final loss label at that instant.

7. **The deadline-label lag cannot automatically inherit O1’s “missing data means loss” fallback.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.3 deadline-label discussion and A4-D reasonableness; `proofs.md` §1.8, Corollary 1.3 Steps 0–2.

   **Why it is wrong:** Corollary 1.3 assumes bounded revelation of the exact deadline labels. That assumption is stronger than merely having a fixed deadline for liquidation attempts.

   If an entry was sold profitably but its sale record is missing, setting both deadline labels to 1, following O1’s fallback, would give
   \[
   L^{lo}=1>L^{liq}=0,
   \]
   destroying the ordering used in Step 0. The statement that the pair is determined “at the sale” also requires complete, final cost and distribution information, not merely a fill notification.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Either explicitly require complete records by \(t+d_D\), or define distinct conservative missing-data labels: lower label 0 and upper label 1 whenever the liquidation outcome cannot be certified. Re-establish the ordering for those revised labels.

8. **The partial-fill liquidation policy leaves execution and allocation rules unspecified.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.1 PF, §3.2 LP.

   **Why it is wrong:** “Submit a sell order for the entire remaining quantity” does not determine an order-and-fill policy without order type, price instructions and submission timing. With overlapping entries in the same security, account-level partial sales also need an allocation rule identifying which entry positions were sold.

   Corporate actions occurring during an extended liquidation delay require position updates beyond O1’s interval \((t,t+H]\). Those updates are not fully specified for LP.

   These choices affect liquidation dates, position-level proceeds, \(S_{ti}\), and all three labels in Corollary 1.3.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Specify the order instructions, allocation of fills and charges across entry lots, and corporate-action handling throughout the complete holding period. Alternatively, make those specifications explicit external inputs required before applying the results.

9. **The nonzero-cash-return wealth formula is false for the adopted start-of-period cost timing.**

   **Location:** `theorems.md` Lemma 3, paragraph following W4; `proofs.md` §3.2 item 7.

   **Why it is wrong:** The statement claims, under W1–W3,
   \[
   W_{T+1}-W_1=\Pi+\sum_t\rho_tW_t.
   \]
   But the adopted convention debits fees and execution shortfall at the start of the period. Writing their sum as \(c_t\), the later derivation in the proof gives
   \[
   W_{t+1}=(W_t-c_t)(1+\rho_t)+h_t^\top x_t.
   \]
   Therefore
   \[
   W_{T+1}-W_1
   =\Pi+\sum_t\rho_t(W_t-c_t),
   \]
   not the stated formula.

   Counterexample: initial cash 100, start-of-period charge 1, cash return 10%, no risky holdings. Terminal wealth is 108.9. The stated formula gives a wealth increase of 9 instead of 8.9.

   The proof acknowledges a timing correction parenthetically, but the theorem does not impose end-of-period costs.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Use the corrected formula for start-of-period costs, or explicitly impose end-of-period debiting. With mixed payment times, account for the corresponding cash returns. The zero-cash-return specialization does not have this discrepancy.

10. **The fixed-time comparison silently treats a random clock as deterministic.**

   **Location:** `proofs.md` §1.5, “Cost of stitching.”

   **Why it is wrong:** Minimizing
   \[
   \frac{L}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda v}{8}
   \]
   gives \(\sqrt{vL/2}\) for a **deterministic** clock value or deterministic upper bound. Here \(V_{j,m}\) is generally random and adaptive. Selecting \(\lambda\) after observing it is not justified by a fixed-\(\lambda\) exponential bound, even at a fixed calendar time.

   Thus the discussion cannot generally interpret \(\sqrt{V_{j,m}\log(2/\delta)/2}\) as an available fixed-time confidence radius in the model being studied.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Label this an algebraic comparison with a deterministic-clock benchmark. State explicitly that it is not a fixed-time guarantee obtained by substituting the realized adaptive clock.

   Relatedly, “a mixture boundary would address (α) only” is too categorical: a mixture changes the quantitative boundary and its logarithmic penalties as well. It does not generally eliminate the need for a time-uniform penalty, but it need not preserve the particular \(L_k\) used here.

11. **O1-close is not fully defined by “otherwise as above.”**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §3.2, O1-close variant.

   **Why it is wrong:** V1–V4 explicitly refer to the opening auction, the official opening price and suspension at the open. Reusing those rules does not define a closing valuation. A security suspended at the open but trading by the close is one immediate ambiguity.

   Also, saying its P&L differs by “the open-to-close return” omits position scaling and possible changes in modeled sale costs.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Provide a closing-price hierarchy and its availability assumptions. Under otherwise identical accounting, express the P&L difference as the change in marked position value minus the change in hypothetical sale costs.

12. **The accounting formulas use unstated price and corporate-action restrictions.**

   **Location:** `theorems.md` Lemma 3, share-based trade definitions and return shortcut; `proofs.md` §3.2 item 2.

   **Why it is wrong:** The statement allows \(p_t\in\mathbb R^m\), but the subsequent formulas divide by \(p_{t-1}\). The proof additionally uses
   \[
   h_{t-}/\phi_t,
   \]
   requiring nonzero share multipliers. Arbitrary dollar holdings also cannot be represented as \(s_t\circ p_t\) on an asset with zero price and nonzero holdings.

   The proof introduces \(p_t>0\), but this restriction is absent from the accounting statement. Elementwise multipliers also do not represent general cross-security conversions or mergers.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Restrict the displayed shortcut to assets with strictly positive prices and positive share multipliers. State that its corporate-action model covers only multipliers and cash distributions; use an explicit holdings transformation for other actions. These restrictions concern the accounting interpretation, not identity (2).

13. **The split examples use the wrong split terminology.**

   **Location:** `theorems.md` Lemma 3; `proofs.md` §3.2 item 2.

   **Why it is wrong:** A share multiplier of 2 with price changing from 100 to 50 is a **2-for-1** split. A **1-for-2** split is a consolidation with multiplier \(1/2\).

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Replace “1-for-2” with “2-for-1” wherever the multiplier is 2.

14. **The explanation of A4-D imposes a restriction absent from its formal definition.**

   **Location:** `setup.md` §4, A4-D reasonableness.

   **Why it is wrong:** The text says A4-D excludes ledgers that record one component before the other. The formal assumption allows exactly that: choose \(J_T\) to contain only entries for which both components are known, and ignore singly known components in these counts.

   **Fixable:** Yes.

   **Suggested fix:** Say that the **interval calculation** uses paired information through a common set; the underlying ledger may record components separately.

The operational delay assumptions, synthetic execution model, missing-data loss convention and accounting timing are substantive additional assumptions. They cannot be justified by describing A1 as “true by construction” or A4 as mere bookkeeping. The issues above concern their implementation, consistency and interpretation, together with ancillary comparison claims.

**Verdict: FIXABLE ERRORS.**
