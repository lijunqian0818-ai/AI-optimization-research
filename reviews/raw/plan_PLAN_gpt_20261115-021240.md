**Verdict: major revision before confirmatory experiments.** The plan is unusually explicit about limitations, but several unresolved issues change the estimands themselves. The baselines are a credible starting set, tuning fairness is not yet established, and the synthetic-validation section is a claim map rather than an executable validation protocol.

The strongest elements are the point-in-time data audit, separation of submissions from fills, O1/O2 distinction, delayed-label training, and explicit allowance for inconclusive results. Keeping the monitoring interval out of method comparisons is appropriate.

This review assesses the supplied plan; it does not independently verify the referenced theorem statements or exchange rules.

1. **Critical — Q-select is not consistently defined, and the confirmatory tests do not consistently test it.**  
   **Relevant sections: §7–§10, §15.2.**

   Q-select is introduced as gate-versus-stratified-RND performance. The H-A confirmatory tests instead use the aggregate Lemma 3 composition term. These are different quantities: aggregate composition can contain cohort timing, sector tilts and other effects that the matched comparison is intended to separate.

   The H-Z contrasts are also ambiguous. “Ungated(+Z) versus ungated(-Z) under Q-select” could mean:
   - a direct strategy difference;
   - a difference between each strategy and its own RND control;
   - a common-exposure comparison.

   Those alternatives answer different questions. Moreover, matching submissions does **not** ensure matched invested exposure: fills, blocked exits and accumulated sleeve wealth differ.

   **Required fix:** Give each confirmatory test an explicit formula, comparator, weighting convention and null hypothesis. Distinguish within-cohort selection from aggregate composition. Treat submission-matched RND as a comparison under matched order opportunities, not exact exposure matching. Use a separate standardized cohort comparison to study selection at fixed initial dollars.

2. **Critical — The proposed daily attribution may not reconcile with the actual ledger.**  
   **Relevant sections: §3.7, §8.2–§8.3, §13.**

   Lemma 3 needs holdings measured at a common valuation time and a common asset-return vector. The plan combines start-of-day holdings with close-to-close returns for continuing positions, open-to-close returns for entries, and close-to-open returns for exits. A stock can be an entry in one strategy and a continuing or exiting position in another. There is then no single daily stock return \(x_t\) serving all strategies.

   A zero residual from an algebra implementation would not establish reconciliation with independently computed cash flows.

   **Required fix:** Split each day into overnight and intraday intervals, with auction trades between them. Apply attribution separately on each interval, then sum. Reconcile against an independently calculated ledger including dividends, corporate actions and terminal holdings.

   Also qualify the proposed “PERF cohort composition must be zero” test: it requires proportional cohort holdings throughout their lifetime. Do not classify every violation as a coding bug before establishing those conditions.

3. **Critical — The primary performance metric and the inferential target are different.**  
   **Relevant sections: §8.3, §9.1, §10–§10.1.**

   P1 appears to mean annualized compounded portfolio return. Attribution and power calculations instead use additive P&L divided by initial capital. These are not interchangeable, and the composition terms do not add to a CAGR difference.

   Daily dollar P&L divided by initial capital also changes scale as wealth compounds, complicating the proposed stationary-bootstrap interpretation.

   **Required fix:** Define separately:
   - terminal wealth and CAGR for deployed strategies;
   - additive P&L attribution;
   - the precise statistic used for each hypothesis test and power calculation.

   The bootstrap, equivalence bound and power calculation must target the same statistic.

4. **High — NET is not a clean architecture comparator, and its RND control is underspecified.**  
   **Relevant sections: §6.1, §7–§8.2.**

   NET selects from \(C_t\), while LOSS selects only within the base top-K. A NET-versus-LOSS comparison changes both architecture and available opportunities. NET’s matched RND pool is unclear: sampling from the base top-K would not match NET’s opportunity set.

   NET coverage is also undefined consistently: exceeding a threshold and taking at most K names from \(C_t\) is different from accepting a fraction of K nominated names.

   **Required fix:** Add a NET-on-base-top-K baseline. Retain full-universe NET as a separate end-to-end benchmark. Define its coverage denominator, randomization pool, training population and cost-adjusted target explicitly.

5. **High — Cheap, relevant baselines are missing from round 1.**  
   **Relevant sections: §6.1–§6.3.**

   The most important additions are not RL or neural selective prediction. They are:
   - direct top-\(cK\) selection by base score;
   - volatility, liquidity and market-trend filters;
   - a feasible volatility-targeting or exposure-control strategy;
   - a simple regularized loss classifier.

   CONF’s monotone isotonic calibration may largely reproduce score thresholding, but ties and global thresholds make the direct score baseline worth specifying separately.

   E-const uses an evaluation-window exposure estimate. It is useful as an ex-post attribution reference, but not an implementable policy chosen before that window. Its scaling factor could also exceed one, conflicting with the no-leverage constraint.

   **Required fix:** Move the cheap controls into round 1. Add an E-const whose scaling is fixed using development data, and label the ex-post version separately. Round 2 can remain optional.

6. **High — Equal trial counts do not establish fair tuning.**  
   **Relevant sections: §4.3–§4.4, §6.3.**

   Thirty configurations provide neither equal optimization effort nor comparable search-space coverage. Two-stage systems also receive base-model and gate optimization, whereas NET receives one stage. A return-rank-trained base plus loss-trained gate differs in objective from NET as well as architecture.

   The hold-out protocol needs clarification: annual validation on Y−1 would use hold-out outcomes for Y≥2023, yet §4.4 prohibits hold-out hyperparameter retuning. Early stopping, probability calibration and threshold recalibration are also adaptive choices.

   **Required fix:** Freeze an explicit annual deployment algorithm identifying what may update using matured past labels. Separate model fitting, hyperparameter selection, calibration and threshold setting. Report search spaces and compute, and include a small development-only tuning-budget sensitivity. Log-loss cannot be the common criterion for all listed model types.

7. **High — Several execution variants violate the declared information timeline.**  
   **Relevant sections: §3.1–§3.2, §3.7, §11, §16.2.**

   Same-auction purchases sized using realized sale proceeds or fill outcomes cannot generally be submitted as fixed pre-auction orders. The “strict” variant uses the day’s close to decide whether an opening trade filled. That uses future information relative to \(\tau_t\), even if intended as conservative.

   Full-day VWAP execution also changes the decision/fill filtration and potentially the revelation-lag argument.

   **Required fix:** Specify pre-auction order quantities and funding. Treat close-based fill exclusions as hindsight diagnostics, not feasible strategies or theorem-valid streams. Define a separate timeline for VWAP execution.

8. **High — Z’s interpretation exceeds what the controls identify.**  
   **Relevant sections: §5.4, §7, §15.2.**

   R contains selected summaries, not the complete price/turnover history consumed by Z. It omits exact windows and ordering information used by the chip recursion; longer Z variants make the mismatch larger. A positive result would establish incremental value over **these summaries**, not over arbitrary flexible functions of the same raw history.

   In cell (1,0), the gate receives the +Z base score. Thus “gate has no Z” means no direct Z features, not no Z information. Comparing (0,1) and (1,0) also changes nominated names and score distributions.

   **Required fix:** Narrow the interpretation to a pipeline-feature intervention. Add matched-window history controls, fixed-nomination placement experiments, and capacity-matched nuisance-feature controls. Fit any Z residualization using training data only.

9. **High — The synthetic protocol is insufficient to test the stated guarantees.**  
   **Relevant sections: §9.4, §12, §15.2.**

   Listing “time-uniform coverage” is insufficient. The experiment must evaluate whether the target lies inside the interval **simultaneously at every eligible time**, not just at the terminal date.

   **Required additions:**
   - Explicit data-generating processes with analytically known conditional targets.
   - Adaptive selection based on observed history, variable \(n_t\), zero-entry periods and stopping.
   - Outcome-dependent revelation satisfying the assumed delay bound.
   - Strong within-day and cross-day dependence, including the Proposition 2 construction.
   - Separate checks for both interval forms, the one-sided bound and deadline-label intervals.
   - Fixed simulation horizons, replication counts and binomial uncertainty for estimated failure probabilities.

   Slight empirical coverage below \(1-\delta\) is not automatically a proof or code failure; Monte Carlo uncertainty matters. Scaling experiments illustrate Proposition 2’s construction—they cannot establish its universal lower bound.

   Finally, compare against at least one valid dependence-aware comparator under matching assumptions. An invalid naive Wald interval alone is too weak a benchmark.

10. **High — Equivalence and negative-result language are inconsistent.**  
    **Relevant sections: §9.3, §10, §15.2.**

    A 90% interval inside the equivalence margin implements an individual 5% TOST, not automatically the stated Holm-adjusted family. Define an equivalence p-value as the maximum of the two one-sided p-values, then apply the family procedure consistently.

    More seriously, §15.2 allows “Z adds nothing” when no contrast differs significantly from zero. That contradicts the plan’s own distinction between equivalence and low power.

    **Required fix:** Reserve “economically negligible” for demonstrated equivalence; otherwise report “no detectable improvement” or “inconclusive.” Resolve bootstrap block lengths for every H: length 20 fails the stated minimum at H=10 and H=20, and length 10 fails even at primary H=5.

11. **Moderate — Tail-risk and dependence metrics need technical corrections.**  
    **Relevant sections: §9.1, §9.5, §10.**

    - Marking unresolved trades does not produce realized O2 returns. Report that tail metric as a marked hybrid, alongside unresolved counts and resolved outcomes.
    - Equal-weight trade metrics can overrepresent small trades; add capital-weighted versions.
    - Trade-level expected shortfall cannot be recovered by averaging daily expected shortfalls. Bootstrap date blocks containing the underlying trade records and recompute the pooled tail statistic.
    - An ICC “on day-demeaned labels” removes the between-day component needed for the usual one-way ICC.
    - With variable daily counts, loss-rate variance must respect the ratio \(\sum X_t/\sum n_t\).
    - Independence and Proposition 2’s construction are reference scenarios, not universal bounds within which empirical effective sample size must lie.

12. **Moderate — Data validation needs adversarial accounting cases, not only agreement thresholds.**  
    **Relevant sections: §2.4, §3.5–§3.6, §5.**

    Agreement between vendors sharing upstream data is not independent validation. Aggregate pass rates can hide errors concentrated in distress events—the events most relevant to loss avoidance.

    Add targeted checks for delistings, terminal recoveries, long suspensions, dividends and share changes. Specify how turnover above one is handled in decay weights, how initial chip mass is set, and how warm-up requirements change for the 500-day and 260-week variants. A 2008 start cannot support all proposed feature variants throughout the earliest evaluation years.

**Minimum additional experiments before the hold-out**

Prioritize these over expanding the neural/RL baseline list:

- Independently reconciled execution/accounting stress tests, including same-stock entry/exit across strategies and corporate actions.
- Known-truth synthetic coverage tests with simultaneous failure measurement.
- Matched-opportunity NET versus LOSS and direct score selection.
- Cheap risk/exposure controls with deployment-feasible calibration.
- Fixed-nomination and matched-history Z ablations.
- A complete-null pipeline simulation to check whether tuning, matching, bootstrap inference and multiplicity produce the advertised false-positive behavior.

The plan can support a careful study of attribution and incremental feature value. In its current form, however, ambiguous estimands and accounting conventions would make even statistically precise results difficult to interpret.
