# Review of Experiment Plan Amendment v0.9.3 (Part 1 & 2: Header, A0–A4.2)

**Recommendation:** **Reject / Major Revision Before Any Hold-Out Unblinding.**

---

### Executive Summary

Amendment v0.9.3 is remarkably candid about its empirical fragility: it explicitly concedes that the study is severely underpowered, that its confidence intervals will likely be wider than the equivalence margin $\Delta$, that its primary contrasts cannot settle economic materiality, and that the empirical experiments test **nothing** about the theoretical results. 

However, intellectual honesty about fatal limitations does not cure them. The amendment formalizes an experimental dead-end: it proposes burning an unblinded 4.5-year hold-out dataset on a 12-contrast simultaneous inference framework that is mathematically predestined to be non-informative. Furthermore, the shift from "abstention" to "fixed-coverage re-ranking" creates profound misalignments between the stated research goals, the tuning criteria, the model loss functions, and the primary evaluative metrics.

Below is the detailed review evaluating baselines, metrics, theoretical alignment, missing experiments, and a ranked hierarchy of issues by severity.

---

### 1. Are the Baselines Strong and Fairly Tuned?

**No. The baselines suffer from asymmetric tuning objectives, confounded opportunity sets, and omitted low-complexity benchmarks.**

1. **Asymmetric Tuning Objective in P1 and P4 (`SCORE` vs. `LOSS` / `NET-K`):**
   - The primary baseline `SCORE` is generated by a base model tuned on **RankIC** across the broad candidate universe.
   - In contrast, the secondary gate `NET-K(-Z)` (and `LOSS(-Z)`) has its hyperparameters selected on the **primary evaluation endpoint $V^g$** (mean net return per submitted order) during the tuning years (§A2.2).
   - This creates an immediate tuning asymmetry: if `NET-K` outperforms `SCORE`, the study cannot distinguish whether the gain originates from the two-stage gating architecture or merely from tuning hyperparameters directly against $V$ rather than RankIC.
2. **Confounded Opportunity Sets in P5 and P6 (`BASE-V`):**
   - P5 and P6 introduce `BASE-V` (a single-stage model tuned on $V$). However, §A4.1 notes: *"Nominations differ between the two sides: BASE-V submits its own best $\lceil c \cdot K \rceil$ names; the daily submitted counts are equal, the opportunity sets are not."*
   - By allowing `BASE-V` to nominate its own candidates from the universe, P5 and P6 test a compound hypothesis: candidate selection differences **plus** re-ranking differences. This destroys clean attribution to the gating mechanism.
   - The document dismisses a fixed-nomination `SCORE-V` baseline by claiming it is *"what NET-K(-Z) already is (P4)"*. This is false: `NET-K` is an auxiliary model trained on out-of-sample base nominations, whereas `SCORE-V` would simply be the base model scored on those same nominations with hyperparameters tuned on $V$.
3. **Absence of Simple, Non-Learned Baselines in the Primary Family:**
   - The primary family (P1–P6) is exclusively ML-vs-ML or ML-vs-SCORE. 
   - Pilot facts (§A1.2) revealed that simple heuristics like `VOL` (lowest 20-day volatility) and `LIQ` (highest liquidity) had substantial selection activity ($\iota = 0.29$) and that stratified `SCORE` lost half its edge. Yet `VOL`, `LIQ`, and simple linear shrinkage models are completely relegated to secondary/exploratory status. A skeptical reviewer must see a simple rule-of-thumb baseline in the primary family.
4. **Decayed Base Predictor Degrading Baseline Quality:**
   - §A1.5 reports that candidate-set RankIC decayed to 0.03–0.04 in 2021, with nomination spread dropping to near-zero. If the base nominations in the hold-out contain zero alpha, evaluating re-ranking is merely measuring variance and noise filtering on an uninformative candidate pool.

### 2. Are the Metrics Appropriate?

**Partially appropriate in accounting rigor, but fundamentally mismatched with the model objectives and the original "loss gate" premise.**

1. **Mismatched Objective Function vs. Metric:**
   - The gate `LOSS` is trained on a binary 0/1 loss label (classifying whether an order loses money). 
   - Yet, the primary metric across all primary contrasts (P1–P6) is strictly **$V^g$ (mean net return per submitted order)**. 
   - Evaluating a binary classification model solely on mean net return ignores trade-offs in variance, tail-risk mitigation, and loss frequency. The plan admits in §A0(v) that the study cannot claim gates make a strategy "lose less often" because loss-frequency statistics are relegated to exploratory analysis. If the primary metric is return, training a 0/1 loss classifier is structurally misspecified.
2. **Distortion from Unfilled Orders Imputed as $\rho = 0$:**
   - Unfilled orders are assigned $\rho = 0$ and counted in the denominator $S_t^g$. 
   - While this prevents conditioning on execution, it introduces an asymmetric market bias: during market downturns, an unfilled order with $\rho = 0$ artificially inflates $V^g$ relative to filled losing trades. If a gate disproportionately selects names that fail to fill in down-limit or illiquid market regimes, it achieves a spurious performance boost.
3. **Semantic Incoherence: Fixed-Count "Abstention":**
   - The plan acknowledges (D9) that a fixed-count rule ($\lceil c \cdot K \rceil$) cannot decide "whether to trade today" or reduce exposure on bad days. 
   - While the authors rename the primary study to "fixed-coverage re-ranking", retaining policies named `LOSS` and comparing them to "abstention" in secondary claims is a cosmetic compromise. The metric $V^g$ measures within-day cross-sectional sorting, not downside abstention.
4. **Pooled Ratio ($V^g$) vs. Time-Averaged Return:**
   - In §A2.2, $V^g$ is computed as a pooled ratio ($\sum \rho / \sum S$) rather than the equal-weighted mean of daily or annual performance. High-volume or high-nomination periods will dominate the metric, weighting certain market regimes unequally. *(Noted as PLACEHOLDER in text, pending user confirmation).*

### 3. Do the Experiments Actually Test the Stated Theorems and Claims?

**No. The empirical experiments are completely detached from the theory.**

1. **Explicit Theoretical Disconnection:**
   - §A0 states unconditionally: *"What the study cannot show, whatever the outcome... (iv) anything about the theory results (§A11)... No contrast of this family uses a theory result (§A11)."*
   - Having theoretical propositions and theorems in a paper that are explicitly untestable by the primary empirical pipeline turns the theory into decorative mathematics.
2. **P2 and P3 Cannot Test Feature Group Informativeness:**
   - P2 ($\delta(\text{LOSS}(+Z_H), \text{LOSS}(-Z))$) and P3 ($\delta(\text{LOSS}(+Z_O^*), \text{LOSS}(-Z))$) purport to test the value of holder state ($Z_H$) and participant action ($Z_O^*$).
   - As admitted in §A0(iii), these contrasts only evaluate adding features to a specific binary loss gate on top of base nominations. If the underlying `LOSS` gate is ineffective or misspecified, an informative feature could yield an empirical null or negative return difference simply due to estimation noise or objective mismatch.

### 4. What Experiments Are Missing?

*(Note: Material in parts 3–5, such as §A4.3, §A5, §A6, §A7, and §A8–A11, is not visible in this document part. Cross-referenced items are marked as "not visible in this part" where applicable).*

1. **Fixed-Nomination One-Stage Benchmark (`SCORE-V`):**
   - An isolated benchmark where the base model is evaluated on the exact same nominated set but with hyperparameters tuned on $V$ rather than RankIC. Without this, P4 and P5 conflate tuning metrics with model depth.
2. **Score-Level Ensembling vs. Decision Averaging:**
   - §A2.3 explicitly specifies that inference targets the average performance of five separately trained policies (averaging daily realized returns), rather than ensembling their continuous prediction scores. 
   - In quantitative finance, ensembling predicted probabilities/scores prior to top-$\lceil cK \rceil$ selection significantly suppresses seed variance. Testing whether score ensembling beats single-seed averaging is a critical missing baseline.
3. **Primary Downside and Risk-Adjusted Metrics:**
   - Downside metrics (CVaR, maximum drawdown, realized loss rate, Sortino ratio) are absent from the primary confirmatory family. Given that the policies are designed around loss prediction, downside risk metrics belong in the primary confirmatory claims, not merely exploratory cards.
4. **Coverage Sensitivity ($c$):**
   - The primary framework fixes $c = 0.5$ without justification. (Sensitivities to $c \in \{0.3, 0.7\}$ are referenced in §A4.3, which is *not visible in this part*, but fixed-coverage at $c=0.5$ in all primary contrasts is arbitrary).

### 5. Ranked Issues by Severity

#### Tier 1: Critical / Fatal Flaws (Blockers for Hold-Out Unblinding)

1. **Pre-Ordained Statistical Power Collapse (Design Futility):**
   - *Description:* With pilot standard errors of 3.0–3.8 bp, 12 simultaneous contrasts across 4.5 years yield confidence interval half-widths of 6–11 bp (or wider under the max-statistic bootstrap quantile $q \approx 2.5\text{--}2.8$). 
   - *Impact:* The minimum detectable effect at 80% power is 8–14 bp (and 13–19 bp to exceed $\Delta = 4.96$ bp), while observed pilot effects are $-6.1$ to $+1.7$ bp. The experiment is mathematically doomed to produce wide intervals containing zero and $\pm \Delta$. Unblinding a single-use hold-out on an experiment designed to produce an uninformative null is an irrecoverable waste of data.
2. **Violation of Fundamental Bootstrap Assumptions by Annual Refitting:**
   - *Description:* The inferential validity of the block bootstrap requires stationarity (M) and strong mixing (Dp). 
   - *Impact:* The plan itself admits that annual refitting introduces persistent model errors within each year and structural distribution shifts as the training window grows. Resampling 24-day blocks across annual refit boundaries treats a piecewise non-stationary process as a stationary short-memory series, invalidating nominal 95% coverage guarantees.
3. **Complete Disconnect Between Theory and Empirical Setup:**
   - *Description:* The paper disclaims any link between the empirical results and its theoretical theorems.
   - *Impact:* The paper risks bifurcating into unrelated theoretical and empirical components, violating the core standard of scientific coherence.

#### Tier 2: Major Methodological & Baseline Biases

4. **Confounded Comparators in P5 and P6 (`BASE-V`):**
   - *Description:* Comparing a two-stage re-ranking pipeline against `BASE-V` with differing nomination sets prevents isolating the contribution of the second-stage gate.
5. **Asymmetric Tuning Objective in P1 and P4:**
   - *Description:* Comparing RankIC-tuned base scores against $V$-tuned gates attributes tuning metric gains to gating architecture.
6. **Objective Mismatch: Evaluating Binary Loss Classifiers on Mean Net Return:**
   - *Description:* Using $V^g$ as the sole primary metric for a 0/1 `LOSS` gate fails to capture downside mitigation, guaranteeing suboptimal performance against return-maximizing models.
7. **Score Ensembling Omitted:**
   - *Description:* Averaging realized decision ratios across seeds instead of ensembling prediction scores injects unnecessary sampling variance into the estimator.

#### Tier 3: Minor / Observational Issues

8. **Asymmetric Zero Imputation for Unfilled Orders:**
   - *Description:* Assigning $\rho = 0$ to unfilled orders can introduce regime-dependent distortions in falling markets.
9. **Conditional Family Size Depending on Free-Data Audit ($Z_O^*$):**
   - *Description:* Making the confirmatory family size contingent on whether LHB/BLK pass an announcement-date audit introduces post-hoc protocol instability.

---

### Reviewer Verdict

**Do not approve amendment v0.9.3 for freezing or hold-out unblinding.** 
The plan honestly documents its own inability to reach conclusive findings, but honesty does not substitute for statistical power and experimental alignment. The author must either:
1. Re-introduce variable-coverage/risk-aware gates evaluated on downside metrics where loss gates have theoretical validity, or
2. Resolve the baseline asymmetries (`SCORE-V` on identical nominations; score-level ensembling) and reduce multiplicity before expending the unblinded hold-out data.
