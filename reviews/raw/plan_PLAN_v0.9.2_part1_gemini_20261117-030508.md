# Skeptical Review: Experiment Plan Amendment v0.9.2 (Part 1: Header, A0–A3)

---

## Executive Summary & Overall Verdict

**Verdict: REJECT / MAJOR REVISION BEFORE IMPLEMENTATION OR FREEZE**

As a skeptical reviewer evaluating Part 1 (Header, §A0–§A3) of the v0.9.2 amendment, this document reads like an **orderly retreat designed to insulate an empirical failure from falsification**. 

Following the exploratory pilot results—which revealed that the original threshold gates degenerated into erratic timing mechanisms, that the fixed-count loss gate underperformed the simple base score by $-6.07$ bp per order on LightGBM, and that the base model's signal collapsed in 2021—the amendment fundamentally transforms the study:
1. **Abstention has been redefined as fixed-count re-ranking**, eliminating the very mechanism (market timing and dynamic risk reduction) that justifies abstention.
2. The study has been defensively reclassified as an **"estimation study" (§A0)** under the pre-acknowledged reality that statistical power is near zero and that all primary contrasts will almost certainly return **"inconclusive at $\pm\Delta$"**.
3. **The theory is completely decoupled from the empirical testing** (§A0, §A3.2, §A11), rendering the theoretical results purely ornamental.
4. **Tuning and baselines remain systematically asymmetric**, favoring the gate models over the base ranking comparator.

An "estimation study" whose expected outcome is an array of confidence intervals spanning from substantial negative losses to positive gains, wider than the pre-specified economic bound $\Delta$, provides neither actionable engineering guidance nor valid scientific inference.

---

## 1. Are the Baselines Strong and Fairly Tuned?

### 1.1 The Primary Baseline (SCORE) Suffers from Metric Asymmetry
* **The Asymmetry:** In §A2.2 and §A7, gate models (`LOSS`, `NET-K`) are hyperparameter-tuned specifically to maximize $V^g$ (the pooled mean net return per submitted order over the tuning window). Conversely, the comparator baseline `SCORE` is generated by a base model tuned on **RankIC** (cross-sectional Spearman correlation of raw returns across all candidates).
* **The Bias:** The gate is optimized directly on the downstream objective function and restricted to the nominated set, whereas `SCORE` is optimized on a rank-correlation proxy across the full universe. Evaluating `SCORE` on $V^g$ places the baseline at an unfair disadvantage.
* **The Strength:** Paradoxically, despite this handicap, `SCORE` beat `LOSS(-Z)` by $+6.07$ bp in the pilot on LightGBM. If `SCORE` were fairly tuned on $V^g$ (or if a single-stage ranking model were trained on net returns), the gate's putative utility would likely deteriorate further.

### 1.2 Omission of Natural Linear and Shrinkage Baselines
* The fixed-count rule compares complex nonlinear machine learning gates (`LOSS`, `NET-K`) against raw `SCORE`, `VOL`, and `LIQ` in isolation.
* In quantitative asset management, the standard baseline for combining a primary alpha score with risk factors (volatility, liquidity) is a **penalized linear model (Ridge/Lasso) or simple heuristic rank blend** (e.g., $z(\text{Score}) - \lambda z(\text{Vol})$). 
* Without a simple linear/shrinkage re-ranking baseline, any marginal gain of `LOSS(+Z)` over `SCORE` cannot be attributed to the "loss gate" architecture or $Z$ features, but rather to basic linear exposure adjustments that a simple multi-factor model achieves without complex gating.

### 1.3 Baseline Degeneracy in CONF
* In §A2.1, the authors note: *"CONF keeps the threshold rule of v0.8 (a fixed-count CONF is identical to SCORE)."*
* This is a critical admission: the standard baseline for selective classification (confidence/margin-based rejection) is mathematically identical to `SCORE` under fixed-count ranking. Rather than addressing how confidence estimation should function under fixed-count selection, `CONF` is sidelined into the legacy threshold framework, removing the most classical abstention baseline from the primary fixed-count comparison.

## 2. Are the Metrics Appropriate?

### 2.1 The Primary Metric ($V^g$) Ignores Downside Risk and Tail Loss
* **Metric:** $V^g = \frac{\sum_t \sum_{i \in \text{Sub}_t^g} \rho_{ti}}{\sum_t S_t^g}$ (mean net return per submitted order).
* **Flaw:** A "loss gate" is ostensibly built to **mitigate losses and curtail downside risk**. Yet $V^g$ is purely an expected return metric. It is completely blind to variance, maximum drawdown, semi-variance, and tail loss. 
* A gate that selects high-volatility, lottery-like stocks with a high mean return but catastrophic fat-tail risk will be ranked superior by $V^g$ over a gate that successfully stabilizes the portfolio. Relegating loss frequency ($\Lambda$) and tail metrics to exploratory, unadjusted secondary status (§A0, §A4.3) creates a fatal misalignment between the stated problem (loss prevention) and the optimizing metric.

### 2.2 Arbitrary Treatment of Unfilled Orders ($\rho_{ti} = 0$)
* In §A3.1, unfilled orders enter the denominator with $\rho = 0$.
* While this penalizes submitting unfillable orders (e.g., limit-up halts), treating unfilled orders as exact zero-return trades distorts the metric:
  1. It conflates **execution failure** with **economic performance** (a zero-return trade is treated identically to an executed trade that broke even).
  2. If a policy selects names prone to trading suspensions or limit moves, the denominator dilutes the measured return, but does not capture the opportunity cost or cash drag associated with locked collateral.

### 2.3 Per-Order Metric Disconnected from Portfolio Capital Reality
* $V^g$ is denominated per submitted order of 1 yuan of slot cash. With holding period $H = 5$ days, submitted orders interact over time through overlapping holding windows.
* Equal daily order counts ($S_t^g = \lceil c \cdot n_t \rceil$) do not imply equal capital utilization, equal turnover, or equal portfolio Sharpe ratio. Demoting deployed annualized returns ($\Delta AR$) and portfolio risk metrics to "descriptive" hides the portfolio-level reality.

### 2.4 Flaws in Hyperparameter Tuning Pooling (§A2.2)
* Tuning $V^g$ via the **pooled ratio** ($\frac{\sum \rho}{\sum S}$) across all tuning years weights high-activity days/years more heavily than low-activity periods.
* In financial time series with strong non-stationarity and regime shifts (such as the 2017–2021 Chinese A-share market), this pooling overfits the gate's hyperparameters to the specific high-volume / high-volatility years, degrading out-of-sample stability.

## 3. Do the Experiments Actually Test the Stated Theorems and Claims?

### 3.1 Total Decoupling of Theory and Empirical Pipeline
* **The Admission:** In §A0, the document explicitly states that the study cannot show:
  > *"anything about the theory results (§A11)"*
* In §A11, this is reinforced:
  > *"No primary contrast, key secondary or classification of this amendment uses a result of `theory/theorems.md`."*
* **The Reality:** Theorem 1, its corollaries, and Proposition 2 are relegated to "monitoring tools" or background context. The empirical experiments (P1–P4) are standard tabular ranking comparisons that neither validate the assumptions nor test the bounds of the theoretical derivations. The paper presents two disjoint projects bound together under one title.

### 3.2 The Conceptual Bait-and-Switch: From "Abstention" to "Fixed-Count Re-Ranking"
* **Theoretical Premise:** Abstention is founded on Chow's reject option: an agent abstains when conditional risk exceeds a threshold, reducing exposure during unfavorable market regimes.
* **Empirical Execution:** Under the fixed-count rule (§A2.1), the strategy *must* submit exactly $\lceil c \cdot n_t \rceil$ orders every single day.
  - On a crash day where every candidate has an 80% loss probability, the gate is forced to submit half of them.
  - On a surging bull day where every candidate has a positive alpha, the gate is forced to reject half of them.
* By enforcing $S_t = \lceil c \cdot n_t \rceil$, the authors have eliminated selective market exposure. The experiment does **not** test abstention; it tests **two-stage rank filtering**.

### 3.3 The "Estimation Study" Defense and Underpowering
* Section A0 concedes that with a bootstrap SE of 3.0–3.8 bp and critical value $q \in [1.96, 2.73]$, the minimum detectable effect for "materially beneficial" ($L > \Delta$) is **13.4–18.5 bp per order** (approx. 2.7–3.7% annualized).
* Meanwhile, the exploratory pilot point estimates range from **$-6.1$ to $+1.7$ bp**.
* The authors explicitly acknowledge: *"The most likely outcome is therefore that all eight primary contrasts are 'inconclusive at $\pm\Delta$'."*
* Framing the study as an "estimation study" where inconclusive results are acceptable does not rescue the methodology. A study knowingly designed with statistical precision too coarse to distinguish between severe economic drag ($-6$ bp) and modest economic gain ($+2$ bp) relative to $\Delta = 4.96$ bp is **fundamentally underpowered**.

## 4. What Experiments Are Missing?

1. **End-to-End Single-Stage Baseline Tuned on $V^g$:**
   - A single base model (LightGBM/ridge) trained directly on net return or with an asymmetric loss penalty, tuned on $V^g$ with the same evaluation budget ($T=30$). This is necessary to isolate whether a *two-stage gating architecture* provides any marginal benefit over a properly tuned *single-stage ranking model*.
2. **Abstention Fraction ($c$) Sensitivity Curve:**
   - The plan arbitrarily fixes $c = 0.5$ (median split). Does the gate show signal when filtering out the worst 10% ($c = 0.9$) or selecting only the top 10% ($c = 0.1$)? Evaluating only $c=0.5$ tests a single arbitrary point on the selection curve and may mask selective efficacy in the tails.
3. **Dynamic / Soft Thresholding Without All-or-Nothing Degeneracy:**
   - The pilot's threshold rule failed because of poor probability calibration across varying market volatility. A well-designed abstention experiment would test cross-sectional quantile thresholds or temperature-scaled confidence thresholds rather than abandoning variable abstention entirely for a rigid daily fixed count.
4. **Ensemble Evaluation vs. Average of Seeds:**
   - Section A2.3 specifies inference on the *average performance of five un-ensembled models*. In actual quantitative deployment, one blends the predictions of the 5 seeds into an ensemble. Evaluating un-ensembled averages artificially inflates execution variance and standard errors, needlessly worsening the power deficit.
5. **Direct Ridge / Linear Combination Baseline:**
   - A simple re-ranking baseline combining base score, historical volatility, and liquidity via penalized linear regression.

## 6. Actionable Recommendations

1. **Halt Hold-Out Execution Until Power Deficit is Resolved:**
   - Do not burn the pristine hold-out (2022–2026) on an experiment pre-calculated to yield eight "inconclusive" labels.
   - Ensembling the 5 seeds and expanding the cross-sectional evaluation can compress the standard error from $\sim 3.5$ bp down toward an actionable threshold.
2. **Restore True Abstention via Calibrated / Quantile Thresholds:**
   - Replace the rigid fixed-count rule ($S_t = \lceil 0.5 n_t \rceil$) with a cross-sectionally ranked quantile gate or a rolling Platt-scaled probability threshold that allows the strategy to hold cash on toxic days.
3. **Harmonize Tuning Objectives:**
   - Either tune the base model on $V^g$ as well, or introduce a single-stage LightGBM baseline trained directly on net return to fairly benchmark whether the two-stage gate adds value.
4. **Promote Downside & Loss Metrics to Primary:**
   - Incorporate downside semi-variance or loss frequency ($\Lambda$) into a composite primary metric (e.g., Sortino-type ratio or mean return conditional on tail risk constraint), aligning the evaluation with the core claim of a "loss gate".
5. **Explicitly Position the Paper or Drop Unused Theory:**
   - If the paper is an empirical evaluation of two-stage re-ranking algorithms, remove Theorem 1 from the core claims or design synthetic experiments that directly test the validity of the theoretical bounds under real-world delays.
