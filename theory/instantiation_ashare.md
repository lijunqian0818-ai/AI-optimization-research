## 2. Execution model, and what daily bars cannot support (items X1, X2)

### 2.1 Orders (A1-G)

On each day the strategy submits at most one buy order per name and at most M orders. The list of names, the quantities, the order type, the limit price and the capacity cap of I-§2.2 (E4) are computed by code that reads only data stamped before submission: daily data up to and including day t−1 and public pre-open information of day t (trading calendar, announced corporate events, announced suspensions, the day's price limits). Nothing from the day-t auction or later may enter. The cap M is enforced on submitted orders. This is what A1-G asks for; it is an implementation requirement to be audited, not a fact.

### 2.2 Whole-position execution model EM — a modelling assumption

**Order specification (part of the execution model).** Every buy order is a **limit order priced at the day's upper price limit P^up**; every sell order is a limit order priced at the day's lower price limit P^down; both are entered in the opening call auction only. P^up and P^down are computed from pre-open public information of day t (the day's reference price and the band in force), so the order price is G_t-measurable.

**What the rule text gives [rule: verified, I-§9 (2)].** In a call auction all trades are executed at one price, and the match price is one at which every buy order priced above it and every sell order priced below it is executed in full; time priority decides only among orders priced exactly at the match price (`rule_verification.md` A3). A buy order priced at P^up is therefore filled in full **by rule** whenever the auction produces a match price strictly below P^up, whatever its place in the queue (mirror statement for a sell order at P^down). The order price is thus what gives the full fill its basis in the rule text; replacing it by another limit price changes the execution model. Queue position matters only when the match price equals the order price, i.e. at the limit — the case excluded by (E3).

**What remains an assumption.** (i) Our own order does not change the match price; the cap (E4) stands in for this and the rules guarantee nothing. (ii) The exchanges' maximum order sizes are not modelled. EM is the rule:

> A buy order for stock i in the opening auction of day t (a limit order at P^up) is **deemed filled in full at the opening-auction price** if (E1) the stock is not suspended for the opening auction of day t; (E2) that auction produced a match price; (E3) the match price is **strictly below the order's limit price P^up**; (E4) the order size does not exceed a capacity cap computed from pre-submission data. Otherwise it is **deemed not filled at all** and is cancelled (not carried to a later day). Sell orders (limit orders at P^down): the same with "strictly above P^down" in (E3).

Under EM there are no partial fills and one entry is one whole position ("lot") with a single entry price. Under (E1)–(E3) the full fill follows from the rule text; what EM adds is assumption (i) and the cap (E4). (E3) never credits a fill at the limit price. The same model must be used for every strategy compared and for every label. The fill rate and the sensitivity to (E3), (E4) are to be reported.

### 2.3 Case (a): timestamped auction data are available — the executable definition

Required data: for every name and day, the opening-auction match price and matched volume (or the fact that there was no match), with timestamps, and suspension notices as published before the open. Then (E1)–(E3) are evaluated from records stamped no later than the auction match τ_t, and (E4) from G_t. With

  F_t := all information up to and including the opening-auction match of day t,

A_{ti} is F_t-measurable in the literal sense, the orders are G_t-measurable, and A1, A1-G refer to information that existed at the stated instants. What remains an assumption is EM itself (whole fills) and the absence of other look-ahead in the pipeline.

### 2.4 Case (b): only daily bars — "retrospective synthetic fills"

A daily bar (open, high, low, close, volume, suspension flag, previous close, adjustment factor) is produced after the close. The following facts are **not available at the opening-auction match** and cannot be recovered from a daily bar:

1. whether the opening auction itself produced a match. The published open is the first trade of the day **[rule: verified, I-§9 (3)]**: the 09:25 auction price if that auction matched; otherwise the first trade of the continuous session, at any time of the day; and for a stock suspended at the open and resumed during the day, the price of the **resumption auction**. The daily field is therefore called "the published open", never "the auction price". The smoke test confirms that the bar does not show which case applies (AUDIT_REPORT A12), and that the vendor flags intraday suspensions as ordinary trading days (A22, 4 of 4 checked);
2. whether the stock traded at all on day t, when the auction had no match;
3. the volume matched in the auction (never in daily bars), so (E4) cannot be compared with auction liquidity;
4. intraday suspensions and resumptions; what a vendor's "suspended" flag covers is vendor-specific (smoke test, bar source BaoStock: the flag means whole-day suspension; n = 28, below the formal audit's requirement);
5. the day's high, low, close and total volume;
6. what the data looked like on day t: a file downloaded later is "as of the download date" and may contain corrections.

Consequently an entry indicator built from "the stock traded on day t and its open was not at the limit" is **not** measurable with respect to information at the auction match, and taking d_label = H + 1 on the label side does not repair look-ahead on the entry side. If daily bars are used, the following is the definition, and it must be called by its name:

> **Synthetic fill rule SF.** Orders are generated as in I-§2.1 (G_t only) with the order specification of I-§2.2: a buy order is a limit order at the day's upper price limit P^up. An order for stock i on day t is recorded as a **synthetic fill** in full at the **published daily open** of day t if (S1) the daily bar of day t exists, has positive volume (an empty volume field counts as zero) and is not flagged as a whole-day suspension; (S2) the published open is **strictly below the order's limit price P^up**; (S3) the order size is within the cap of (E4). Otherwise it is recorded as not filled and cancelled. Sell side (limit order at the lower price limit P^down): "strictly above P^down" in (S2). SF uses, of the day-t bar, **only** the traded flag, the suspension flag and the open — the fields that coincide with auction-time facts whenever the auction produced a match — and never the high, low, close or volume size.

*Notes on (S2).* (i) P^up and P^down are computed from the day's reference price and the band by board, date and status; the bands are **[rule: verified, changed in sample, I-§9 (5)]** and the dated calendar is PLAN §3.10 (RC-5, RC-6). (ii) The earlier wording of (S2) was "the daily open is not the upper price limit". On an ordinary day the two forms coincide, because the open cannot exceed the limit. They differ on days on which a seasoned stock trades without a price limit (I-§9 (5)) and on days with a wrong reference price: there the open can lie above the computed P^up, and the earlier wording would have recorded a fill above the order's own limit price. The present form records "not filled". With it SF needs no calendar of no-limit days. Whether an order priced at the computed P^up would have been accepted on a no-limit day was not checked; such days are counted, not modelled. (iii) The rule basis of (S2) is the call-auction principle of I-§2.2; it holds for the auction and says nothing about a published open that came from the continuous session or from a resumption auction (item 1 above).

> **Declared information set under SF.** F_t^syn := all data dated ≤ t−1, the pre-open public information of day t, and the day-t open, traded flag and suspension flag of all names. It is increasing in t. It is an **artificial** information set: on days on which a name had no auction match it contains facts that became known only later that day.

What can and cannot be said under (b):
- With respect to (F_t^syn), the synthetic entries satisfy A1 provided the rest of the pipeline has no look-ahead, and the O1 label of I-§3 satisfies A3 with d = H + 1 **as a function of the downloaded file** (I-§4). Theorem 1 then gives a valid interval for the target r_T^entry **of the synthetic fill stream, defined with F_t^syn**.
- That is **not** a statement about an executable strategy. A real order may not have filled where SF records a fill (no auction match, limited auction volume, queue position) or may have filled at a different price; μ_t is conditional on F_t^syn, not on what a trader knew at the match; and A1-G holds for the orders but the fill filter is retrospective.
- The size of the discrepancy between SF and real auction fills cannot be estimated from daily bars. It needs at least a sample of auction data (I-§9, unresolved).
- In any write-up the object must be called "synthetic fills under SF" and the filtration named as F_t^syn; the phrase "information at the opening-auction match" may be used only in case (a).

## 3. Primary label O1: the valuation rule as a decision tree (item X4)

**Label.** L^mark_{ti} = 1 if *net proceeds at the mark* < *cost basis*, else 0 (ties are not losses). Cost basis = shares acquired × entry benchmark price (the day-t opening price) + buy-side explicit charges + modelled buy-side slippage. Net proceeds at the mark = position value at the mark (below) − sell-side explicit charges − modelled sell-side slippage, both computed as if the whole position were sold at the mark. *(v0.8 sync, T-3.)* "As if sold" refers to the security part q·m of the position value only: the cash part D bears no sell-side charge and no slippage, and after S-d (no security remains) none is deducted. Whether a cash exit carries a tax or fee for the holder was not checked; the plan reports a sensitivity with explicit charges on the cash amount. The dividend-tax total of I-§5 is unaffected. Accounting: benchmark-price accounting, every cost once (`theorems.md` Lemma 3; I-§8 item 8). O1 is a **valuation**; it does not say the position could have been sold.

**Snapshot.** All steps below read one data snapshot only: the monitor's data **as of day t + d_label** (d_label = H + 1). Nothing that arrives later changes the label (A2(b)). **Global override:** if at any step an input needed to evaluate that step is absent from the snapshot, the outcome is **M** ("missing input"): L^mark := 1, flagged. *(v0.8 sync, T-6.)* A listed security without a row in the bar data on a market trading day (after the plan's repair rule, PLAN §3.11 rule 12) is treated as not trading that day. If the mark day t+H has no row, or if branch P1 would have to carry a close across a day without a row (so that the carried close cannot be shown to be the last one), the input is missing: outcome M.

**Step 1 — security transformations and listing status over (t, t+H].** List, from the snapshot, every corporate event of the held security with entitlement date or effective date in the holding window, in chronological order, and classify each:

| Class | Event | Effect on the holding state (security j, shares q, cash D) |
|---|---|---|
| S-a | cash dividend to which the position is entitled (I-§5) | D increases by q × net cash per share |
| S-b | bonus shares, capitalisation of reserves, split, consolidation, with published multiplier φ > 0 | q := q·φ |
| S-c | rights issue to which the position is entitled (it holds the shares at the close of the rights record date R; R is **read from the event record, never derived from the ex-date**) | **conservative convention:** not subscribed, the rights lapse and are valued at zero; q unchanged, D unchanged (no subscription payment, no new shares). The share price is valued ex-rights from day R+1 on — rule R-P1 below. Basis and limits: note on S-c below |
| S-d | the security is removed against **cash only** on published terms | holding becomes cash: D += q × cash per share; no security remains |
| S-e | the security is exchanged for **exactly one** listed successor security at a published ratio | j := successor, q := q × ratio; allowed **at most once** per entry. *(v0.8 sync, T-4.)* After a succession effective on day d, the successor's events are classified by the same entitlement rule as any holding (record date ≥ d); an event of the successor whose ex-date is d itself cannot be classified safely ⇒ **U** |
| U | anything else (spin-off, mixed consideration, conversion, capital reduction, several successors, …), an event that cannot be classified from the snapshot, **or any S-d / S-e event of a successor security** | outcome **U** ("unsupported"): L^mark := 1, flagged; stop |

*Note on S-c (rights issues).* What the sources give (`rule_verification.md` B1, B3; AUDIT_REPORT §3.2): rights certificates are credited to holders at the end of the record date R and can be used only to subscribe during the payment period; no arrangement for transferring them appears in the clearing-house guide or in the announcements read, **but no clause stating that rights may not be transferred was found** — the zero value is a convention, not a verified fact. **A rights issue is the exception to "ex-date = the trading day after the record date"**: by the company announcements read, the stock is suspended during the payment period (about R+1 to R+5) and the following settlement days, and ex-rights trading starts only afterwards (about R+7) if the issue succeeds; the two rights issues in the smoke test had 6 suspended trading days between record date and ex-date. The rule clause for this timing was **not found [rule: tbv]**; the dates are therefore taken from the event record of each issue, never from a rule of thumb. A rights issue whose record date, subscription price or ratio is not in the snapshot gives outcome M. *(v0.8 sync, T-5.)* "Not in the snapshot" is applied to the entries whose window could contain the missing record date: the ex-date is known and lies in (t, t + H + 10 market trading days] (the 10 is the plan's placeholder), or no date is known and the entry is on or after the earliest date the record carries. The plan's event table admits a rights record only if it carries a record date or an ex-date; a record with neither date is left out and listed, and if such an issue was carried out, its ex-rights step of the reference price has no event and gives U by the note on class U below; for such an issue rule R-P1 item 2 cannot be applied (plan-level reading; PLAN §3.8 (iii) note (10)). The convention is conservative for the holder: a holder who does not subscribe bears the whole ex-rights fall of the price and receives nothing for the right.

*Note on class U (plan-level reading).* The experiment plan treats a step of the exchange's reference price for which no event record can be found ("unexplained reference-price step", PLAN §3.11 list URS) as "an event that cannot be classified from the snapshot": outcome U for the label. Its ledger keeps valuing such a name at market prices; that is a ledger convention of the plan and not part of this tree. *(v0.8 sync, T-8, T-10.)* Two further plan-level readings of "cannot be classified from the snapshot", both conventions of the plan's event table (PLAN §3.11), applied mechanically and counted: (a) **date conflicts** — where an event source and the bar data disagree on the day of a price change and the plan's rules cannot settle it, the event is applied on the day of the price step and every entry whose window (t, t+H] contains a day between the two dates gets **U**; (b) **unconfirmed events** — the plan builds its event table without hand checks; an event that is neither confirmed by two sources nor confirmed by the exchange's reference price on its ex-date is not booked and gives **U** for every window containing its ex-date. Because the table is built from one downloaded file, these readings make the label a function of that file (I-§2.4 item 6), like every other input.

After the events are applied: if no security remains (S-d), go to Step 3 with position value D. Otherwise check the **listing status** of the current security j at day t+H: if it is no longer listed and this was not handled by S-d / S-e, the outcome is **U**. Because listing status is resolved here, a delisted security can never reach the "no trade" or "suspended" branches of Step 2.

*(v0.8 sync, T-7.)* *Where S-d / S-e records come from, and what "no longer listed" means (plan-level reading).* The free event sources carry no record of cash exits or share-exchange absorptions; the plan uses a hand-built **delisting disposition table** (PLAN §3.11 rule 11): termination ⇒ U; cash-only exit on published terms ⇒ S-d; exchange into exactly one A-share at a published ratio ⇒ S-e, **assuming that the holder does not exercise any cash option**; anything else, incomplete terms, or a name without a row in the table ⇒ U (the plan's default, if the table is not collected, writes off every delisting). A security is "no longer listed" from the market trading day after its last row in the bar data; the vendor's delisting-date field is not used for this (it is the last row for most names and the first missing day for some). A successor that is not yet listed on day t+H gives **U**, as Step 1 requires.

*What "listed" means, by period* (**[rule: verified, changed in sample, I-§9 (8)]**; current SSE Listing Rules read, the change of 2020-12-31 from an official press Q&A, the 2012 and 2014 texts and the SZSE Listing Rules' delisting chapter not opened):
- A stock trading in a **delisting-arrangement period** is listed and goes to Step 2.
- **Before 2020-12-31 a stock in "suspension of listing" is still listed** (it does not trade, typically for about a year). In this tree it is a suspended security: **branch P1, carry-forward value — not outcome U.** It becomes U only if it is finally removed without S-d / S-e by day t+H. Consequence, stated because a reader would otherwise assume that delisting risk is entirely in U: some positions in names that were later delisted receive a carry-forward mark and possibly the label "no loss". The legal status cannot be read from the free data; **P1 counts are therefore reported by the length of the suspension spell at the mark day** (PLAN §3.5), with a tabulation labelled "hindsight" of the long spells that ended in delisting.
- **From 2020-12-31, trading-type delistings have no arrangement period**: between the delisting decision and the removal the stock does not trade again. A held position is P1 while the stock is still listed at day t+H and U once it is removed.

*Termination.* The window is finite, the event list is finite, at most one succession is allowed, and every unclassifiable case exits through U; the procedure therefore ends with exactly one of: a tentative value, U, or M.

**Step 2 — price rule for the (listed) security j at the mark instant, the opening auction of day t+H.** Exactly one of four mutually exclusive cases holds:

| Case | State on day t+H | Per-share mark m |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | suspended for the opening auction (whole-day suspension or one covering the auction; later intraday resumption ignored). Includes "suspension of listing" before 2020-12-31 and the payment-period suspension of a rights issue | the last closing price at which j traded before the suspension, restated to ex-entitlement terms by **rule R-P1** below |
| P2 | not suspended; the auction produced a match | that auction price, also when it equals a price limit |
| P3 | not suspended; no auction match; the stock traded later on day t+H | the published opening price of day t+H = the first trade of the day, from the continuous session or from a resumption auction **[rule: verified, I-§9 (3)]** |
| P4 | not suspended; no trade at all on day t+H | the day's pre-open reference price: the previous close, or on an ex-date the exchange's ex-rights / ex-dividend reference price, which is what the exchange displays as the previous close that day **[rule: verified, I-§9 (4)]**. It is read from the data source's reference-price field and never recomputed from event records (the issuer may have obtained a different formula) |

The predicates of P3 and P4 ("traded later", "no trade all day") are known only at the close of day t+H. With daily bars P2 and P3 cannot be told apart; both are implemented as "the published open of day t+H when the stock traded that day". This is admissible because the published open equals the auction price whenever the auction matched **[rule: verified, I-§9 (3)]**. With the vendor data of the smoke test a day with an **intraday** suspension is an ordinary trading day (not P1), and its published open may come from the resumption auction. Tentative position value = q·m + D.

**Rule R-P1 — restatement of the carried-forward price in branch P1** (rewritten in the post-audit sync; items S-3, S-5 of the log). Let c be the last market trading day before the suspension on which j traded, and m the unadjusted closing price of day c (c ≥ t, since the entry itself traded on day t). The events of j are processed in chronological order:

1. *S-a / S-b event with ex-date e, c < e ≤ t+H:* m := (m − g) / φ, where g is the **gross (pre-tax)** cash dividend per share (0 if none) and φ the share multiplier (1 if none). This is the exchange's ex-dividend reference-price formula without the rights term **[rule: verified, I-§9 (4)]**. *Gross, not net:* the price is restated by the amount by which the exchange lowers the reference price, which is the pre-tax dividend; the holder's cash D receives the **net** amount (I-§5). The earlier text ("subtract the cash dividend per share already added to D") used the net amount; it overstated the value of a position marked while suspended across an ex-date by the tax on one dividend.
2. *S-c event with record date R, c ≤ R ≤ t+H − 1* (the mark day lies after the record date and the stock has not traded since) — **convention P1-R:**

   m := min{ m, (m + ρ·P_r) / (1 + ρ) },

   where P_r is the subscription price and ρ the **announced** number of rights shares offered per existing share, both taken from the issue terms in the snapshot. Nothing is added to q or D (S-c: not subscribed).
3. If an input of 1 or 2 is absent from the snapshot, or the restated m is not positive: outcome **M**.
4. *(v0.8 sync, T-2.)* *Order.* Events are applied in the chronological order of their effective instants: an S-a / S-b event takes effect at the open of its ex-date, an S-c event at the close of its record date. Hence an S-c with record date R precedes an S-a / S-b with ex-date R+1, and when the ex-date of an S-a / S-b event and the record date of an S-c event fall on the same day, item 1 is applied before item 2.

The vendor's carried-forward price on suspended-day rows is **not** used: in the smoke test it had been adjusted during the suspension in 17 spells and was left cum-rights in both rights-issue suspensions inspected (AUDIT_REPORT §6 item 9).

*Why P1-R, and what it is.* Without item 2 a position marked inside the payment-period suspension of a rights issue is valued at the cum-rights close of the record date, although under S-c it no longer carries the right; that overstates its value (`rule_verification.md` B3 (ii)). The expression (m + ρP_r)/(1 + ρ) is the exchange's reference-price formula with the announced ratio in place of the realised change ratio. It is a weighted average of m and P_r, so it is below m exactly when P_r < m, and then it decreases in ρ: with the announced (maximal) ratio it is **not above** the formula's value for any realised ratio between 0 and ρ, including a failed issue (no dilution, value m). The min covers a subscription price at or above m. P1-R is therefore a deliberate **lower valuation, a convention and not an estimate**: (a) the realised ratio and whether the issue succeeds are not known during the payment period; (b) the exchange's own formula uses the change ratio of tradable shares, which need not equal the per-share offer ratio, and the issuer may apply for another formula, so "not above the exchange's reference price" is exact only for the standard formula with a change ratio not above ρ; (c) the reference price is not a price at which the position could have been sold. A more exact treatment — waiting for the first ex-rights trade — is rejected because of the lag (I-§4). Free data cannot support more: the realised subscription outcome is not in the sources tested, and whether a vendor's "ratio" field is the announced or the realised one is unchecked (I-§9).

*Scope of P1-R.* It applies only in branch P1. Once the stock has resumed, the mark is an actual ex-rights price (P2 / P3) or the exchange's own reference price (P4), and no restatement is made. The convention ignores the outcome of the issue even where the snapshot already contains it. Entries marked under P1-R are **counted separately**, as are those of them whose issue later failed (a hindsight tabulation).

**Step 3 — validation at the frozen deadline.** The tentative value is accepted only if **every** input used in Steps 1–2 and in the cost model (event records and their amounts, status facts, the price, fee and slippage parameters, the tax schedule of I-§5) is present in the snapshot. Otherwise the outcome is **M**, which **overrides** the tentative value. If accepted, L^mark := 1{net proceeds < cost basis}.

**Rules around the tree.** (1) No entry is dropped: U and M are losses, not missing values. (2) No revision after day t + d_label. (3) Report the number of entries per branch (S-c, S-d, S-e, P1–P4; P1 by length of the suspension spell; P1-R separately) and, separately, the numbers of U and M; if U + M are not rare the label is driven by a convention and the result must say so. (4) **No vendor adjustment factor and no vendor-adjusted price is used in any label or in the accounting; holdings are tracked as shares and cash.** The earlier text allowed a vendor factor "after the data audit has shown that it encodes exactly S-a–S-c". The smoke test showed the opposite for the two vendors tested: the BaoStock and Sina factors include cash dividends (the adjusted price is a total-return index, not an ex-distribution price), and the BaoStock table lacks 11 events of one code-change name and has 5 factor steps on days without any event (AUDIT_REPORT §3.2, A5 / A23 failed; Eastmoney adjusted prices could not be tested). The condition is not met and the option is withdrawn.

**Limits.** (i) The tree can detect a missing **field**; it cannot detect an event that is absent from the vendor's event calendar. Completeness of the calendar is a data-audit matter; the smoke test found single-source calendars incomplete (the Eastmoney dividend table was empty for 12 of 12 delisted names; AUDIT_REPORT A21), so the plan merges several sources and uses steps of the reference price as a backstop (PLAN §3.11). (ii) "L := 1" for U and M, "rights not subscribed, valued at zero" and the lower valuation P1-R are **my choices**; they are conservative and they turn data problems into recorded losses. (iii) I do not claim that A2 holds "by construction": it holds if the tree is implemented exactly as written and the snapshot is point-in-time, which is to be audited.

## 4. Lags (items X3, X5)

- **Adopted: d_label = H + 1 and d = H + 1**, fixed before any data are examined. The label of I-§3 needs facts that are known only at the close of day t+H (P3, P4), so **the operational lag this label supports is H + 1, not H**, also when auction data are available.
- **Availability assumption (OA-next).** Every input of the tree that refers to days ≤ t+H is in the monitor's data before the decision point of day t+H+1. If it holds, the snapshot contains everything and M does not fire. If an input is late, M fires at day t + d_label and the label is still a function of the snapshot. Either way the label is a function of the day-(t + d_label) snapshot; A3 with d = H + 1 then requires that this snapshot be part of the declared F_{t+H+1} — true in case (a) for a point-in-time data store, and in case (b) only in the weaker sense "as a function of the downloaded file" (I-§2.4 item 6). OA-next, and the point-in-time property, are **data-audit items, not established**.
- **Rights-issue suspensions do not change the lag.** A position whose mark day t+H lies inside the payment-period suspension of a rights issue is valued by branch P1 with convention P1-R (I-§3). Its inputs — the last traded close (day c ≤ t+H − 1), the record date, the subscription price and the announced ratio — are all dated no later than the record date R ≤ t+H − 1, provided the issue terms are published by then (the announcements read fix them before R; no rule clause on that timing was verified, so this is part of OA-next). The label is therefore finalised from the snapshot of day t + d_label like every other label: **d_label = H + 1 and d = H + 1 stand, and no label waits for the resumption of trading.** If the terms are missing from the snapshot, the outcome is M (label 1, flagged), again at day t + d_label. The alternative — marking at the first ex-rights trade — was rejected: the resumption day is about R+7 by the announcements read, without a verified rule, and it has no bound that holds for every issue, so that label would have a longer and uncertain lag and A3 with d = H + 1 would fail for it. The cost of the choice is stated in I-§3: P1-R is a deliberately low valuation, counted separately.
- **Monitoring with a larger lag** d′ > H + 1 keeps the same labels (frozen at H + 1) and the same target (setup §4). It is a separate, separately declared analysis; d is never chosen after seeing widths.
- **Variant with lag H — a different label, not adopted.** A lag of H can only be supported by a label that uses auction-time information exclusively. Such a label ("O1-auction") would replace Step 2 by: suspended for the auction → carry-forward as in P1; auction match → that price; **no auction match → the pre-open reference price, immediately, whether or not the stock trades later**; and would freeze at d_label = H. It needs the assumption that the complete auction outcome of every held name is in F_{t+H} at the match (case (a) data only; impossible with daily bars). It has different X_t, μ_t and target from O1 and may never be pooled with it. It is not specified further and not used. **No statement in this project should say that O1 has lag H.**
- H, d_label and d count market trading days, so holidays need no special treatment.

## 5. Cash distributions: one entitlement-based rule for all labels (item X6)

A cash distribution is attributed to a position **if and only if the position holds the shares at the close of the record date**, in the quantity held at that close, **whatever the payment date**.

*Record date and ex-date.* For cash dividends, bonus shares and capitalisations the exchange applies the ex-rights / ex-dividend treatment on **the trading day after the record date [rule: verified, I-§9 (9)]**; the record date may therefore be derived from the ex-date for these events (smoke test: 467 of 467 Eastmoney records agree). **Rights issues are excepted** (I-§3, note on S-c): their record date is read from the event record.

*Amount.* Net amount per share = announced **gross** cash per share × (1 − τ), where τ is a **tax rate fixed in advance as a schedule by record date**:

| Record date | τ | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| before 2013-01-01 | 10% | flat, independent of the holding period. Known only indirectly (repeal clause of the 2012 notice; the 2005 notice itself was not opened) — **[rule: tbv]**. If it is not verified before the freeze, the plan's fallback is 20% throughout (PLAN §3.10) |
| from 2013-01-01 | 20% | the rate for a holding of one month or less under the 2012 and 2015 notices **[rule: verified, changed in sample, I-§9 (10)]** |

*Mechanism — not "withholding".* The earlier text spoke of "a withholding rate fixed in advance". By the notices read, from 2013-01-01 to 2015-09-07 only 5% is withheld at payment and the rest is collected when the shares are sold; **from 2015-09-08 nothing is withheld at payment for holdings of up to one year and the whole tax is collected at the sale.** The total is the same, and every label uses the total: O1 values the position as if it were sold in full at the mark, so the full tax τ on every entitled distribution is deducted there; O2 and the deadline labels deduct it at the sale. An entitled but unpaid distribution counts as a receivable in net proceeds, net of τ. When the cash and the tax actually move matters only for the spendable cash of a ledger; that timing (receivable, amount paid, liability debited at the sale) is specified in PLAN §3.11 and is not needed for any label.

*Not modelled, stated.* The holding-period brackets: with H = 20 trading days, or after a delayed liquidation, the holding can exceed one calendar month and would in reality fall in a lower bracket (10%; for more than a year 5% before 2015-09-08 and exempt afterwards). First-in-first-out across lots of one name in one account. The tax treatment of bonus shares (not checked). The rules read apply to individuals and securities investment funds; other holders were not checked. τ is an external input and its sensitivity is to be reported.

- O1: record dates from day t to day t+H−1 (the position is treated as held through the close before the mark).
- O2 and the deadline labels: record dates from day t to the day before the sale of the shares concerned.
- P&L and attribution (Lemma 3): the same rule; distributions go to the cash ledger and are never trades.

With this rule a position that is sold in full at the mark has the same distributions under O1 and O2. If an entitled amount is not in the snapshot, O1 gets outcome M and the deadline labels are "not certified" (I-§6.4); a sale by itself does not reveal the label.

## 6. Executable liquidation O2, partial fills, deadline labels (items X7, X8)

### 6.1 Liquidation policy LP

*Schedule.* For entry (t,i), a sell order for the entire remaining quantity of that lot is submitted to the opening auction of day t+H and of every later trading day until the remaining quantity is zero.
*Under EM / SF (backtests).* The order of a day is deemed filled in full at that day's opening price if (E1)–(E4) (resp. (S1)–(S3)) hold in their sell-side form, and not at all otherwise. Liquidation is then a single sale. If several lots of the same security are due on the same day, the capacity condition is applied to their **total** and the deemed fill is all-or-nothing for the total, so no allocation among lots is needed. Order type and limit price do not enter the simulation.
*Label.* L^liq_{ti} = 1 if total net proceeds of all sales of the lot, plus entitled distributions (I-§5), minus sell-side charges, are strictly below the cost basis; else 0. **Completion convention:** L^liq := 1 if the lot is never fully liquidated, or if an event of class U occurs before it is. L^liq is thus defined for every entry.
*Corporate events during an extended liquidation.* The classification of I-§3 Step 1 is applied over the whole period from day t to the liquidation day (S-a–S-e update the lot; at most one succession; class U ends the computation with L^liq := 1). *(v0.8 sync, T-9.)* An S-d event ends the liquidation: the lot has become cash on published terms on the effective day, which counts as the day on which the whole lot is liquidated (no sell-side charge, I-§3 "Label"); the proceeds are the cash amount.

The liquidation delay has no deterministic bound (long suspensions, limit-down sequences). **The O2 convention does not guarantee A3 for any fixed lag**; Theorem 1 is not applied to L^liq. The unresolved O2 labels at any time are not a random subsample, so no estimator based on resolved positions only is used.

### 6.2 When O1 = O2

L^mark = L^liq for an entry if the scheduled sale at the opening auction of day t+H is filled for the whole lot at the valuation price (branch P2 of I-§3), and both labels use the same cost basis, charges, accounting and the distribution rule of I-§5. Under EM / SF this is the event that the sell-side conditions hold on day t+H; with daily bars it measures blocked exits **as defined by SF**, not exits that were blocked in the market. Otherwise the labels can differ in either direction.

### 6.3 Partial fills (fill-level data or live trading) — partly specified

Defined here:
- an entry exists if any shares are filled; the lot is the filled quantity; the unfilled remainder of the buy order is cancelled at the end of the auction;
- sales are allocated to lots of the same security **first-in-first-out by entry day**; explicit charges and shortfall of a sell order are allocated to the lots it serves **pro rata to the value sold**; per-order minimum charges likewise;
- labels are lot-level, from total net proceeds; a lot only partly sold at a deadline counts as **not sold**.

**Not defined here — external inputs required before applying the results with partial fills:** the order type and limit-price instruction of the buy and sell orders; the exact submission time within the auction's order-entry window (the window is 09:15–09:25, with no cancellation from 09:20 to 09:25 **[rule: verified, I-§9 (1)]**; the time within it is still an input); whether an unfilled sell remainder is cancelled after the auction or left in the continuous session; the lot rule to apply — **main board and ChiNext: buy orders in multiples of 100 shares, a remainder below 100 shares sold in one order; STAR Market: at least 200 shares per order with no multiple requirement, a remainder below 200 shares sold in one order [rule: verified, I-§9 (12)]** — together with the exchanges' maximum order sizes, which are not modelled; the broker's actual allocation and fee records. Without them LP is not a complete order-and-fill policy and S_{ti}, L^liq and the deadline labels are not determined.

### 6.4 Deadline (bracket) labels for Corollary 1.3

Fix a deadline D ≥ H and d_label^D = d_D = D + 1, before any data are examined.
- S_{ti} = 1 if LP has liquidated the **whole** lot at or before the opening auction of day t+D.
- **Certification** V_{ti} = 1 if S_{ti} = 1 **and** the snapshot of day t + d_label^D contains the complete and final record of that liquidation: every sale (date, quantity, price), every event of the holding period with its amounts, every entitled distribution, and all cost-model inputs; V_{ti} = 0 otherwise (not sold by the deadline, class-U event, or any required record missing). *(v0.8 sync, T-9.)* An S-d at or before the opening auction of day t+D counts as S_{ti} = 1 (I-§6.1). The record is not complete if any event of the holding period could not be booked — an amount missing, an event that cannot be classified (including the plan-level readings of the note on class U), an event of a successor on the succession day — so such an entry has V_{ti} = 0. L^cert_{ti} := 1{net proceeds < cost basis} computed from the certified record.
- L^lo := V·L^cert, L^hi := 1 − V·(1 − L^cert). **Missing data therefore give lower label 0 and upper label 1**, never "both 1": the O1 fallback M is *not* used for these labels (item X7).
- Assumption **CERT** of Corollary 1.3 reads here: a record that passes the completeness test is in fact complete and final, and is computed with the same LP, execution model, accounting and distribution rule as L^liq. In a backtest the sale record is produced by the simulation, so CERT can fail only through wrong or revised market-data inputs; in live trading it is an assumption about broker and custodian records. Not audited.

## 8. Conventions the experiments must adopt

Checklist for the lead to synchronise into `experiments/PLAN.md` (not edited by me). Everything is fixed **before any data are examined**; a later change alters the estimand or voids the guarantee and must be logged.

1. **Two information times.** Orders (names, quantities, order type, limit price, cap) are functions of G_t = data dated ≤ t−1 plus pre-open public information of day t. Entries are determined afterwards. [setup A1-G; I-§2.1]
2. **Which case, stated in the paper.** (a) timestamped auction data: F_t = information up to the opening-auction match; or (b) daily bars: **synthetic fills under SF**, filtration F_t^syn, and every result is described as holding "under the synthetic execution model", not for an executable strategy. [I-§2.3, I-§2.4]
3. **Entry = (deemed or synthetic) fill.** Non-filled orders are not entries and get no label; the fill rate is reported next to every loss rate. Denominator of every rate: executed entries N_T, never matured entries only. One order per name per day; cap M on orders.
4. **Execution model.** EM (E1)–(E4) in case (a), SF (S1)–(S3) in case (b); same model for all strategies and all labels; report sensitivity to the limit and capacity conditions. **Orders are limit orders at the day's limit price (buy at P^up, sell at P^down); this order price is part of the fill rule and may not be changed without changing the model. (E3) / (S2): the match price / published open is strictly below the buy order's limit price (strictly above the sell order's).** SF may read only the traded flag, suspension flag and open of the day-t bar; the fill price is called "the published open". [I-§2.2, I-§2.4]
5. **Primary label O1** by the decision tree of I-§3: Step 1 events and listing status (S-a–S-e, U), Step 2 price rule (P1–P4, with restatement rule R-P1: gross cash dividend; convention P1-R for a mark day inside a rights-issue suspension), Step 3 validation with override M; share-and-cash tracking, **no vendor adjustment factor or adjusted price**; "suspension of listing" before 2020-12-31 is branch P1, not U; counts per branch (P1 by spell length, P1-R and S-c separately), U and M reported separately; no entry dropped. O1-close is not available.
6. **d_label = H + 1, frozen.** Labels are computed from the snapshot of day t + d_label and never revised. [setup A2(b); I-§3, I-§4]
7. **Monitoring lag d = H + 1**, deterministic, never re-chosen after seeing widths; a different d is a separately declared analysis on the same frozen labels. Lanes are residue classes of the market trading-day index. No claim that O1 has lag H.
8. **Benchmark-price accounting, every economic cost exactly once** (lead decision, 2026-11-15). Holdings, pre-trade holdings, returns, cost bases and marks of all strategies (G, B, attribution reference) use the same benchmark price at each instant — the opening price; explicit charges and modelled execution shortfall enter only through C; slippage is never also applied to prices. Trades are share-based: trade_t = (s_t − s_{t−}) ∘ p_t with s_{t−} the pre-trade shares after corporate actions; the return shortcut only with the ex-distribution, share-adjusted price return; P&L with total returns. Costs are debited at the trade (start of period). *(v0.8 sync, T-1.)* trade_t remains the **net** change of the share vector. Explicit charges and modelled shortfall are charged **per executed order**, on the order's benchmark-price value (plus a per-share fee where one applies), and each order's cost is booked once, to its lot; a buy order and a sell order of one strategy in one name on one day are separate orders and are not netted, so the order-level turnover can exceed ‖trade_t‖₁ and both are reported. An attribution reference path has no orders; its notional cost is computed on its net implied trades. Lemma 3 holds for any cost term, so this fixes the value of C, not the identity. [`theorems.md` Lemma 3]
9. **Distributions by entitlement** (holding at the close of the record date, regardless of payment date; gross amount × (1 − τ) with **τ a tax rate fixed in advance by record date: 10% before 2013-01-01 — not yet verified against the original text — and 20% from then; from 2015-09-08 the tax is collected when the shares are sold, not withheld**; labels use the total, the cash timing is the plan's), identically for O1, O2, deadline labels and P&L; cash ledger, never a trade. Record date derived from the ex-date for dividends and bonus issues only; read from the event record for rights issues. Brackets by holding period not modelled (H = 20 and delayed liquidations may in reality pay less). [I-§5]
10. **Pending labels** are counted both ways (P_T in the upper endpoint); the matured-only rate K_T/(N_T − P_T) is not reported as an estimate of anything covered by the theory; "revealed" means the frozen final value is recorded. [setup A4]
11. **O2 and deadline labels.** O2 is not monitored with Theorem 1. If Corollary 1.3 (status `草稿`) is used: D ≥ H, d_D = D + 1 fixed in advance; LP of I-§6.1; "sold" = whole lot; **certification**: missing or incomplete records give L^lo = 0, L^hi = 1; interval computed through a common revealed set J_T. [I-§6]
12. **Partial fills and allocation.** None under EM / SF (same-day lots of one security are treated as one all-or-nothing order). With fill-level data: FIFO allocation to lots, charges pro rata to value sold, and the external inputs of I-§6.3 must be fixed in writing first.
13. **Attribution conventions (Lemma 3)** fixed in advance and reported: fallback weights w^B on zero-baseline-exposure days; ordering of the decomposition; cost model applied to the reference's never-executed trades; Π is called a wealth change only if W1–W4 are verified.
14. **No new entry in a name that is in a delisting-arrangement period** (added in the post-audit sync). Individual buyers face asset and experience conditions there (`rule_verification.md` A9), the same reason for which ST names are excluded. The free data carry no dated flag for the period (the vendor's ST flag is 0 inside it, AUDIT_REPORT A9), so the exclusion can only be implemented by a proxy that is a function of G_t; the plan's proxy is an ST look-back (PLAN §3.5, a placeholder of the plan, with stated misses). The exclusion acts on the candidate set, i.e. on orders; it does not touch any label of a position already held.
15. **Candidate set from a universe that does not use index membership** (added in the post-audit sync). Free data do not give index constituents point-in-time (AUDIT_REPORT A2: vendor snapshots at irregular dates, two suspected late updates, no official list to compare with). The plan therefore proposes a universe defined by a liquidity screen on data dated ≤ t−1 (PLAN §2.3; **pending user confirmation at the time of writing**). Whatever universe is adopted, the requirement of this file is only that membership be a function of G_t (A1-G); the choice changes the population the results describe, not the mapping to the abstract objects.
