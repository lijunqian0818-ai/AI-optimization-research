# Selection process - title/abstract screening

Records retrieved from candidates_for_screening (n = 156) were combined and de-duplicated with the sr-screener skill (version 1.0.0) of the open-source Academic Research Skills suite (https://github.com/Imbad0202/academic-research-skills), running in Claude Code, which matches PMIDs, DOIs and normalised titles with publication year (plus or minus one year); 0 duplicates were removed, leaving 156 unique records. Titles and abstracts were screened against the pre-specified eligibility criteria of the review protocol. Each record was assessed independently by two AI reviewers (both Claude Sonnet 5.5 (claude-sonnet-5-5), prompted with different expert personas) that were given the same written protocol and decision rules and did not see each other's decisions. The reviewer agents had read-only file tools and no web access; each was instructed to read only the file it was given. Disagreements between advancing (include or unclear) and excluding a record (8 records) were resolved by a third AI reviewer (Claude Sonnet 5.5 (claude-sonnet-5-5)) that re-read the record. The two reviewers agreed on advance-versus-exclude decisions for 94.9% of records (Cohen's kappa = 0.53; prevalence-adjusted bias-adjusted kappa = 0.897). Because records excluded by both reviewers are never adjudicated, a reproducible random sample of those joint exclusions, together with exclusions matching keyword signals for the core criteria (5 records in total), was re-screened by a senior AI reviewer (Claude Sonnet 5.5 (claude-sonnet-5-5)) that did not see the earlier decisions; 2 were advanced as a result. All 2 known eligible studies used as a sensitivity check were retrieved by the search and advanced. In total, 3 records were excluded and 153 records (111 judged eligible and 42 with insufficient information) were advanced to full-text assessment.
